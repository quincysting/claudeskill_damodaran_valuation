## 1. Core frameworks and principles

- **The three corporate finance decisions.** Every business makes an investing decision (what to invest in, judged against a hurdle rate), a financing decision (debt vs. equity mix), and a dividend/cash-return decision (how much to return, and as dividends or buybacks) — and the end game is maximizing business **value**, not just profit ([Data Update 5 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-5-for-2024-profitability.html), 2024-01). Damodaran rejects "stakeholder wealth maximization" as a business objective: giving management multiple constituencies to answer to makes it accountable to none, producing "confused corporatism" (2024-01).
- **Danger and opportunity are inseparable.** Damodaran's teaching definition of risk is the Chinese symbol 危機 (danger + opportunity): you cannot pursue the opportunity side of risk without exposure to the danger side, so risk-minimization alone is not a coherent business or investing strategy ([Data Update 4 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-4-for-2024-danger-and.html), 2024-01; reused in [Data Update 5 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-5-for-2026-risk-and-hurdle.html), 2026-02).
- **Diversification and market pricing underlie standard risk/return models.** Almost every risk-and-return model in finance (CAPM included) rests on two joint assumptions: marginal investors are diversified, and prices reflect investor risk views. Reject either assumption and you need an alternative risk measure (accounting-based, or "total risk" instead of just market risk) — but the fix is to build a different measure, not to abandon risk measurement ([Data Update 4 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-4-for-2024-danger-and.html), 2024-01).
- **The cost of capital is "the Swiss Army knife of finance."** It plays four distinct roles depending on context: an *opportunity cost/hurdle rate* for capital budgeting and acquisitions; an *optimizing tool* for the debt/equity mix; a *divining rod* for cash-return decisions (invest below cost of capital → return more cash); and a *risk-adjusted discount rate* for DCF valuation from the outside. It "morphs" depending on which of these roles it's playing ([Data Update 6 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-6-for-2025-from-macro-to.html), 2025-02; [paper: The Cost of Capital](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/papers/costofcapital.pdf)).
- **The corporate (and country) life cycle governs almost every financial variable.** Reinvestment needs, margins, cost of capital uncertainty, dividend capacity, and even a country's growth/stability trade-off all shift systematically as an entity ages — young/high-growth entities take more risk for more upside; mature entities trade growth for stability; declining entities face structural stagnation ([Data Update 9 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/03/data-update-9-for-2025-dividends-and.html), 2025-03; [Data Update 4 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html), 2026-02).
- **Currency invariance theorem.** The value of a project or company does not change depending on the currency you value it in, as long as your cash flow growth assumptions and discount rate are built consistently around the same expected-inflation assumption for that currency. Being wrong about inflation doesn't hurt you if you're consistently wrong in both the numerator (cash flows) and denominator (discount rate) ([Data Update 4 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html), 2026-02).
- **Debt vs. equity is a claims-based distinction, not an accounting one.** Debt = contractual, fixed/floating but pre-specified claim, with priority in liquidation and legal enforceability of missed payments; equity = residual claim with control rights, no fixed maturity, and no legal jeopardy for skipped payouts. Because accountants often mis-categorize (leases were off-balance-sheet debt until 2019; R&D is still expensed rather than capitalized), you must apply this economic definition yourself ([Data Update 8 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-8-for-2025-debt-taxes-and.html), 2025-02; [Data Update 7 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-7-for-2026-debt-and-taxes.html), 2026-02).
- **Dividends should be residual cash flows** — declared only after investment and financing needs are met — but in practice dividend policy is driven by inertia (stickiness) and me-tooism (peer mimicry), which is the central dysfunction of the cash-return decision ([Data Update 9 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/03/data-update-9-for-2025-dividends-and.html), 2025-03; [Data Update 8 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-8-for-2026-time-for.html), 2026-02).
- **Buybacks and dividends are economically identical cash returns**; neither can create or destroy value on its own — a buyback (or dividend) done at a price above or below fair value only **transfers wealth** between shareholders who sell and those who hold, it does not create or destroy firm value ([Data Update 9 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/03/data-update-9-for-2025-dividends-and.html), 2025-03; [Data Update 8 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-8-for-2026-time-for.html), 2026-02).
- **The Fed does not "set" interest rates** in any meaningful sense beyond the very short end. Long rates are driven by fundamentals — expected inflation and real growth (the Fisher equation) — and the Fed's Fed Funds actions have historically followed, more than led, market rates, especially past 2022 ([Data Update 3 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-3-for-2024-interest-rates.html), 2024-01; [Data Update 4 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-4-for-2025-interest-rates.html), 2025-01; [Data Update 3 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-3-for-2026-trust-deficit.html), 2026-01).
- **Trust is the invisible asset underlying markets.** Currency value, bond pricing, and legal enforceability all rest on trust in institutions (central bank independence, government solvency, courts). Loss of that trust doesn't hit all markets equally or immediately — in 2025, US equities and bonds shrugged off tariff/ratings/shutdown/Fed-independence shocks, while the dollar weakened and gold/silver spiked, showing markets are not a monolith on trust risk ([Data Update 3 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-3-for-2026-trust-deficit.html), 2026-01).
- **Moats (excess returns) come from competitive advantage width (strength) and depth (durability), not from financial engineering.** Businesses can't manufacture excess returns by tweaking the discount rate; only real barriers to entry generate returns above the cost of capital ([Data Update 6 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-6-for-2026-in-search-of.html), 2026-02, citing Michael Mauboussin's moat work).
- **The "theocratic trifecta"** — stakeholder wealth maximization, ESG, and sustainability — fail for the same three reasons: they root themselves in virtue rather than business logic (critics get cast as immoral, not wrong); their central terms stay amorphous by design, which makes them easy to sell but incoherent; and they sell the lie that you can "do good" with no trade-off/sacrifice ([Data Update 6 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-6-for-2026-in-search-of.html), 2026-02, referencing the 2024 "Siren Song of Sustainability" post).

## 2. Methods and procedures

**Sampling and data construction**
- Universe: *all* publicly traded companies with a market price > 0 (not S&P 500 or large-cap subsets), to avoid survivorship/size sampling bias. ~47,700 firms (Jan 2024) → ~48,156 firms (Jan 2026), across ~134 countries and ~94–95 industry groupings (Damodaran's own groupings, descended from 1990s Value Line categories, not SIC/NAICS) ([Data Update 1 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-1-for-2024-data-speaks-but.html); [Data Update 1 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-1-for-2025-draw-and-danger.html); [Data Update 1 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-1-for-2026-push-and-pull-of.html)).
- Six broad regions: United States, Europe, Asia ex-Japan, Japan, Australia & Canada, Emerging Markets (with India and China broken out as separate sub-groups because of their size) ([Data Update 1 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-1-for-2024-data-speaks-but.html)).
- **Aggregated ratios, not simple averages.** For any ratio prone to negative/undefined values or outliers (PE, margins, ROE, multiples), Damodaran computes an *aggregated* statistic: sum the numerator across **all** firms (including money-losers) and divide by the summed denominator, rather than averaging individual-firm ratios. This avoids both survivorship bias (excluding negative-earnings firms) and outlier skew (PE ratios are unbounded above but floored at zero). Median/first-third-quartile figures are reported alongside the aggregated number where useful ([Data Update 1 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-1-for-2024-data-speaks-but.html); reiterated every year through 2026).
- **Timing convention**: market data (prices, rates, risk premiums) as of the first days of January; accounting data as trailing-twelve-month numbers through the most recently filed quarter (typically Sept 30 of the prior year) — deliberately mismatched in timing because that's what an investor actually had access to at that point ([Data Update 1 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-1-for-2024-data-speaks-but.html); [Data Update 1 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-1-for-2025-draw-and-danger.html)).
- **Global consistency choices Damodaran deliberately keeps against accounting convention**: leases treated as debt (accounting caught up in 2019); R&D capitalized as an asset, not expensed (accounting still disagrees) — numbers are reported both with and without the R&D adjustment ([Data Update 1 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-1-for-2026-push-and-pull-of.html)).
- Currency: raw dollar figures (market cap) converted to US$ for aggregation; ratios are currency-invariant so no conversion is needed for margins/multiples/returns ([Data Update 1 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-1-for-2026-push-and-pull-of.html)).

**Implied equity risk premium (ERP) — Damodaran's primary market-pricing tool**
- Definition: the internal rate of return that equates the current index level to the present value of expected cash flows to equity holders (dividends + buybacks), grown using consensus analyst earnings estimates for years 1–2, then scaled down to a stable growth rate (≈ risk-free rate) beyond that; net out the risk-free rate to get the ERP ([Data Update 2 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-2-for-2024-stock-comeback.html); [Data Update 2 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-2-for-2025-party-continued.html); [Data Update 2 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-2-for-2026-equities-get.html)).
- Deliberately **model-agnostic and forward-looking**, in contrast to (a) the historical ERP (average of past stock-minus-bond annual returns, which is backward-looking and carries a large standard error — 2.12% on the 1928–2024 averages; 2.05% on the 7.03% 1928–2025 average, i.e., a range from a little under 4% to over 11%) and (b) the earnings yield (E/P) approach, which implicitly assumes either zero earnings growth or that all growth comes from investments earning exactly the cost of equity — both assumptions contradicted by the data (S&P 500 earnings grew 6.58%/yr 2000–2024; S&P 500 ROE was 20.61% in 2023 and averaged 16.38% since 2000, well above cost of equity) ([Data Update 2 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-2-for-2025-party-continued.html); [Data Update 1 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-1-for-2026-push-and-pull-of.html)).
- To value the S&P 500: take current index level, consensus analyst earnings for next 2 years, dividends+buybacks as the cash-flow base, taper growth to the risk-free rate, use the *current* T-bond rate as one input, then solve for fair value at an assumed "reasonable" ERP, or solve for the implied ERP at the current price. Damodaran publishes a spreadsheet each year (linked in each post) so readers can substitute their own growth/payout assumptions ([Data Update 2 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-2-for-2024-stock-comeback.html), [2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-2-for-2025-party-continued.html), [2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-2-for-2026-equities-get.html)).
- Reframe the tie-breaker: whether you think stocks are over/undervalued reduces to whether you think the *current* implied ERP is too low (overvalued), too high (undervalued), or "just right" (fairly priced, if you're not a market timer) ([Data Update 2 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-2-for-2026-equities-get.html)).
- Damodaran runs Monte Carlo simulation (Crystal Ball, an Excel add-on) around the base-case value to get a probability that the market is over/undervalued, rather than a single point estimate ([Data Update 2 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-2-for-2024-stock-comeback.html), [2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-2-for-2025-party-continued.html)).

**Intrinsic risk-free rate (Fisher-equation check on treasury rates)**
- Intrinsic riskfree rate = actual (or 10-yr-average) inflation + real GDP growth rate, compared against the observed 10-year T-bond rate. This tracks the big historical moves in the T-bond rate far better than "the Fed did it" narratives ([Data Update 3 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-3-for-2024-interest-rates.html); [Data Update 4 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-4-for-2025-interest-rates.html); [Data Update 3 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-3-for-2026-trust-deficit.html)).
- Empirical test of "Fed leads markets": short-term T-bill rate changes correlate strongly and significantly with Fed Funds changes; but the correlation between Fed Funds changes and 10-year T-bond changes is weak (~0.08, barely significant) — and reversing the test (does the market lead the Fed?) yields similarly weak/strong patterns, i.e., the evidence for "Fed leads" is no stronger than for "market leads Fed" ([Data Update 4 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-4-for-2025-interest-rates.html)).

**Riskfree rate by currency**
- Start with a government's local-currency bond rate; strip out default risk using the sovereign rating-implied default spread (or CDS spread where traded) to arrive at a currency risk-free rate: `Riskfree rate in currency = Govt bond rate − default spread`. For currencies with no reliable local bond market, use the differential-inflation approach instead: `Riskfree(local) ≈ Riskfree(US$) + [Expected inflation(local) − Expected inflation(US$)]`, compounded properly as `(1+US rf)×(1+infl_local)/(1+infl_US) − 1` ([Data Update 5 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-5-for-2025-its-small-world.html); [Data Update 4 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html) — worked Egyptian-pound example).
- Since May 2025, the US is no longer treated as automatically default-free: Damodaran nets the US's own default spread (0.23% at the start of 2026) out of the implied ERP to get a "mature market" premium, then builds other countries' equity risk premiums on top of that ([Data Update 4 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html)).

**Country/equity risk premium by country**
- Start with the mature-market (US) implied ERP; for non-Aaa countries add a country risk premium scaled from the sovereign-rating-implied default spread (multiplied by a factor to reflect equities being riskier than sovereign bonds); for unrated ("frontier") countries, substitute a country-risk-service score (e.g., Political Risk Services/PRS) ([Data Update 4 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-4-for-2024-danger-and.html); [Data Update 5 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-5-for-2025-its-small-world.html); [Data Update 4 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html)).
- Four broad drivers of country risk: political structure (democracy = more frequent policy uncertainty vs. autocracy = near-term stability but tail-risk of catastrophic rupture), war/violence, corruption (an implicit tax + erodes rule-following), and legal/property-rights protection ([Data Update 4 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-4-for-2024-danger-and.html); [Data Update 4 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html)).

**Cost of capital / hurdle rate estimation (Damodaran's global WACC dataset)**
- Riskfree rate: current US 10-yr T-bond rate (consistently, all firms valued in US$ for cross-comparison).
- Equity risk premium: implied ERP of the country where the firm is headquartered (a simplification — ideally it should be revenue/production-geography-weighted, which Damodaran does for individual-company valuation but not for the industry-average dataset).
- Relative equity risk: **industry-average ("bottom-up") beta**, unlevered and relevered to each firm's own debt ratio — deliberately *not* the regression beta of the individual stock, because bottom-up industry betas have much better out-of-sample explanatory power for returns.
- Default risk/cost of debt: actual bond rating where available; otherwise a synthetic rating from interest coverage ratio, backed up by stock-price standard deviation for the smallest/least-covered firms.
- Weights: market capitalization for equity; total debt including capitalized lease debt for the debt weight (book debt ratios explicitly rejected for cost-of-capital purposes — market value is what it would cost to acquire the firm today) ([Data Update 6 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-6-for-2025-from-macro-to.html); [Data Update 5 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-5-for-2026-risk-and-hurdle.html); [Data Update 7 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-7-for-2026-debt-and-taxes.html)).
- Practical shortcut: for young/hard-to-value companies, use the cost-of-capital **distribution** (e.g., 90th percentile of US firms) as a proxy rather than building up a bespoke number — Damodaran did this valuing Uber in June 2014, using the 90th-percentile US cost of capital (12%) so analytical effort could focus on revenue growth/margins instead ([Data Update 6 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-6-for-2025-from-macro-to.html)).
- Optimal capital structure: the debt ratio that minimizes the overall cost of capital — Damodaran provides a downloadable optimizer spreadsheet (`capstru.xlsx`) that finds this by explicitly modeling how both cost of debt and cost of equity rise with leverage ([Data Update 8 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-8-for-2025-debt-taxes-and.html); [Data Update 7 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-7-for-2026-debt-and-taxes.html)).
- After-tax cost of debt = pre-tax interest rate × (1 − marginal tax rate); it's the **marginal** tax rate (statutory), not the effective or cash tax rate, that determines the debt tax shield, since the shield operates on the last dollar of taxable income ([Data Update 8 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-8-for-2025-debt-taxes-and.html); [Data Update 7 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-7-for-2026-debt-and-taxes.html)).

**Free cash flow to equity (FCFE) / potential dividends**
- FCFE = Net income + Depreciation/non-cash charges − CapEx − Δ Working capital + New debt issued − Debt repaid. Every line item should be recoverable from the statement of cash flows. This is Damodaran's measure of the cash a company *could* return, against which actual dividends+buybacks are compared ([Data Update 9 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/03/data-update-9-for-2025-dividends-and.html); [Data Update 8 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-8-for-2026-time-for.html)).

**Accounting returns and excess returns**
- Return on equity = Net income / Book value of equity; return on (invested) capital = after-tax operating income / (Book debt + Book equity − cash), with Damodaran's own capitalization of leases and R&D built in (reported with and without R&D adjustment) ([Data Update 5 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-5-for-2024-profitability.html); [paper: Return on Capital, ROIC and ROE — Measurement and Implications](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1105499)).
- Excess return = accounting return − matching cost (ROE vs. cost of equity; ROIC vs. cost of capital) — must compare like-for-like (equity-to-equity, capital-to-capital) ([Data Update 7 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-7-for-2025-end-game-in.html); [Data Update 6 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-6-for-2026-in-search-of.html)).
- Coefficient of variation in earnings (std. dev. of 10-year earnings ÷ 10-year average earnings) is Damodaran's preferred earnings-volatility risk metric, since it controls for the scale of earnings across firms ([Data Update 5 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-5-for-2026-risk-and-hurdle.html)).
- HiLo price-range risk measure = (High price − Low price)/(High price + Low price), a simple, liquidity-agnostic complement to standard deviation and beta ([Data Update 5 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-5-for-2026-risk-and-hurdle.html)).

**Small-cap and value premium testing**
- Uses Ken French's return data going back to 1927; computes the premium as the compounded annual return spread between the smallest (or lowest P/B) and largest (or highest P/B) deciles, then re-tests with different starting years to show how fragile/sample-dependent the "premium" is ([Data Update 3 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-3-for-2025-slicing-and.html)).

## 3. Rules of thumb and heuristics

- **Sanity-check a discount rate against the distribution**, not against your gut: a US-dollar cost of capital above ~14–15% for a mature, publicly traded company should go on your "suspect list" (it's above the ~90th percentile); if you're spending most of your valuation time refining the discount rate rather than growth/margin assumptions, "you have lost the script" ([Data Update 6 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-6-for-2025-from-macro-to.html); [Data Update 5 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-5-for-2026-risk-and-hurdle.html)).
- **Know when to stop analyzing.** More data/analysis only helps when the uncertainty you face is *estimation* uncertainty; if it's *economic* uncertainty (the more common case in valuation), no amount of extra digging removes it — accept the analysis is a "work in progress" and decide anyway ([Data Update 4 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-4-for-2024-danger-and.html)).
- **Don't let the discount rate become a dumping ground** for every hope and fear about a company — separate risks that the discount rate should capture (non-diversifiable/market risk) from risks that shouldn't be there (diversifiable, or already reflected in your cash-flow scenario weighting) ([Data Update 4 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-4-for-2024-danger-and.html)).
- **Never use book-value debt ratios for cost of capital or valuation** — only market value; book value is a backward-looking accounting artifact unrelated to what it costs to acquire the firm today ([Data Update 7 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-7-for-2026-debt-and-taxes.html)).
- **Use industry-average ("bottom-up") betas, not company regression betas.** They are noisier at the single-stock level but far better predictors of the cross-section of returns ([Data Update 6 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-6-for-2025-from-macro-to.html)).
- **Base a company's equity risk premium on where it does business (revenue/production), not on its country of incorporation** — the standard-practice shortcut of using country-of-incorporation is something Damodaran "vehemently" disagrees with ([Data Update 6 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-6-for-2025-from-macro-to.html)).
- **Multi-business companies should not use one corporate-wide cost of capital** for every division/acquisition target — doing so cross-subsidizes risky divisions with safe ones and destroys value over time; this is "a myth… with dangerous consequences" ([Data Update 6 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-6-for-2025-from-macro-to.html)).
- **A company's hurdle rate should be dynamic, moving with its life-cycle stage** — high early, tapering toward the market median as growth/uncertainty resolve; a "fixed" hurdle rate across time or a rate set by management fiat rather than market risk is a sign of denial ([Data Update 6 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-6-for-2025-from-macro-to.html)).
- **The small-cap premium and the value (low P/B) premium have not existed, on net, for the past ~35–50 years** (small-cap premium vanishes for any start date from 1970 on; low-P/B premium flips negative after ~1990). Treat "premium is coming back" narratives skeptically, and expect volatility (winning some years, losing others), not a persistent edge ([Data Update 3 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-3-for-2025-slicing-and.html); some partial reversion seen in 2025, see §5).
- **Interest-rate forecasts are only credible if paired with an inflation/growth story** — a bare prediction that rates will rise/fall "because of the Fed" is empty; tie every rate call to a view on expected inflation and real growth, since those two drive the diagnosis of what happens to *value* (inflation-driven rate rises are roughly neutral for pricing-power firms and negative for firms without it; growth-driven rate rises tend to be closer to neutral for the market overall) ([Data Update 4 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-4-for-2025-interest-rates.html)).
- **A rising interest-coverage ratio / falling debt-to-EBITDA = more debt "comfort"/safety**; use interest coverage as the best single explanatory variable for non-financial bond ratings ([Data Update 7 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-7-for-2026-debt-and-taxes.html)).
- **Treat dividends and buybacks as functionally the same decision** — a company that shouldn't be paying dividends (insufficient/uncertain cash flow) shouldn't be buying back stock either, and vice versa ([Data Update 8 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-8-for-2026-time-for.html)).
- **Never build an investing strategy purely around cash-return metrics** (dividend yield, Dogs-of-the-Dow-style high-yield screens) — it structurally selects for mature/declining firms concentrated in a handful of sectors (banks, utilities, real estate) and "misses the essence of equity investing" ([Data Update 9 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/03/data-update-9-for-2025-dividends-and.html); [Data Update 8 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-8-for-2026-time-for.html)).

## 4. Myths and mistakes Damodaran calls out

- **"The Fed sets interest rates."** False beyond the short end; long rates move with inflation/growth expectations, and post-2022 evidence suggests the *market* has been leading the Fed, not the reverse ([Data Update 3 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-3-for-2024-interest-rates.html); [Data Update 4 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-4-for-2025-interest-rates.html); [Data Update 3 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-3-for-2026-trust-deficit.html)).
- **"An inverted yield curve reliably predicts recession."** It's a signal with a great deal of noise — the curve was inverted through all of 2023 with no recession materializing, and by Damodaran's read it can lead the actual event by two-plus years, "what good is a signal that is two or three years ahead of what it is signaling?" ([Data Update 3 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-3-for-2024-interest-rates.html)).
- **"Debt is cheaper than equity, so more debt lowers the cost of capital."** True only to the extent of the tax benefit; absent taxes, more debt raises both the cost of equity (levered risk) and eventually the cost of debt (default risk), leaving the *unlevered* cost of capital unchanged or higher — there's no free lunch ([Data Update 8 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-8-for-2025-debt-taxes-and.html); [Data Update 7 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-7-for-2026-debt-and-taxes.html)).
- **"Borrowing to boost ROE creates value."** Raises ROE but also raises the cost of equity correspondingly; without the tax shield, this is not value creation ([Data Update 8 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-8-for-2025-debt-taxes-and.html)).
- **"Equity is free if you don't pay dividends."** No — the cost of equity includes expected price appreciation; a zero-dividend company still has a real, often higher, cost of equity ([Data Update 8 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-8-for-2025-debt-taxes-and.html)).
- **"Buybacks at overpriced levels destroy value" / "buybacks at underpriced levels create value."** Both false — a buyback at any price is a wealth *transfer* between shareholders who sell and those who hold, not net value creation or destruction. Related myths also rebutted with data: buybacks are no longer just a US phenomenon; they don't reduce aggregate corporate investment (cash is redirected to younger/growth firms, not destroyed); they are not primarily debt-funded (non-financial US debt ratios *fell* over the buyback era, and buyback firms carry *lower* debt loads than non-buyback firms); and reducing share count only raises price per share if done at fair value, since leverage/WACC side-effects can offset the arithmetic ([Data Update 9 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/03/data-update-9-for-2025-dividends-and.html); [Data Update 8 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-8-for-2026-time-for.html)).
- **ESG/sustainability deliver better returns and are good for society.** Damodaran found the evidence "laughably weak" on business outcomes; ESG's apparent historical "alpha" was largely an accidental overweight to tech stocks in its early years; risk-reduction claims are hazy with no discernible cost-of-capital effect ([Data Update 6 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-6-for-2026-in-search-of.html), referencing the 2020 ESG post).
- **"Small-cap and value premiums are timeless and will return."** The data says otherwise for any modern (post-1970/1990) sample window — treat their historical existence as regime-dependent, not a law of markets ([Data Update 3 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-3-for-2025-slicing-and.html)).
- **"Rising net margins are just accounting sleight-of-hand" or "just labor exploitation."** Damodaran is skeptical of both stories — accounting rule changes over the period (expensing stock comp, R&D) should have *pushed margins down*, not up; and margins are stagnant/falling in high-labor-cost sectors (manufacturing, services) even as aggregate margins rise, undercutting a pure capital-vs-labor story. The preferred explanation: market composition has shifted toward tech, which structurally carries better unit economics/scale economics than the old-economy sectors it's displacing ([Data Update 6 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-6-for-2026-in-search-of.html)).
- **"AI productivity gains will make companies more profitable."** Damodaran argues the opposite is more likely if the AI tools are broadly available to competitors: costs fall for everyone, then price competition erodes the margin gain — pointing to online retail's effect on brick-and-mortar margins as the analogy; expects a "slow-motion car wreck" for margins, not a productivity windfall ([Data Update 6 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-6-for-2026-in-search-of.html)).
- **"Private credit's superior default-risk models justify its rates to AI borrowers."** Damodaran is skeptical: for large-cap AI investors (Big Tech) private credit offers no real edge over bank/bond financing on price; its real edge (speed, customization) invites weaker due diligence, and the post expects a "shakeout" separating disciplined lenders from reckless ones ([Data Update 7 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-7-for-2026-debt-and-taxes.html)).
- **Historical-average ERP as a forward estimate.** Backward-looking and carries a standard error large enough (~2%) to make point estimates nearly meaningless without stating the range ([Data Update 1 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-1-for-2026-push-and-pull-of.html)).

## 5. How Damodaran's views evolved (dated)

- **On treating the US as a "default-free" mature-market benchmark:** Standard until Moody's downgraded the US from Aaa to Aa1 on 2025-05-16 (after the close). Since then Damodaran subtracts the US's Aa1 default spread from the T-bond rate to get the US$ riskfree rate (0.40% on 2025-05-30: 4.41% − 0.40% = 4.01%, lifting the US ERP from 4.23% to 4.63%), and takes the same spread out of the US ERP to get the "mature market premium" used for the remaining Aaa countries: 4.48% − 0.27% = 4.21% (July 2025, eleven Aaa countries), 4.46% − 0.23% = 4.23% (start of 2026) ([Sovereign Ratings, Default Risk and Markets: The Moody's Downgrade Aftermath](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/06/sovereign-ratings-default-risk-and.html), 2025-06; [Country Risk 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/07/country-risk-2025-story-behind-numbers.html), 2025-07; [Data Update 2 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-2-for-2026-equities-get.html), 2026-01; [Data Update 4 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html), 2026-02).
- **On the small-cap/value premium:** Called both effectively dead for the 21st century in the 2025-01 post ("the small cap premium has become a large cap premium… for much of this century"). By the 2026-01 post, Damodaran reports a partial reversal within 2025 alone — small-cap and low-P/B stocks returned, at least in part, to favor — while cautioning readers not to read this as the premium's return, but as the start of more volatile back-and-forth between the groupings ([Data Update 3 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-3-for-2025-slicing-and.html), 2025-01 → [Data Update 2 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-2-for-2026-equities-get.html), 2026-01).
- **On US equity concentration (Mag Seven):** Documented the group rising from ~11% of US market cap in 2014 to 30.89% by end-2025, and their 2025 market-cap gain alone equaling 39.3% of the entire US market's increase for the year — while noting they are increasingly *diverging* from each other in performance (Apple/Amazon single digits; Nvidia +37.8%, Alphabet +62.7% in 2025) rather than moving as a bloc, a shift from the more uniform "Mag Seven" narrative of 2023–2024 ([Data Update 3 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-3-for-2025-slicing-and.html), 2025-01 → [Data Update 2 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-2-for-2026-equities-get.html), 2026-01).
- **On international diversification:** After a decade-plus of US outperformance leading many US advisors to write off global diversification as unnecessary, the US underperforming the rest of the world in 2025 (US share of global market cap fell from 48.7% to 46.8%, with China +32.5% vs. India +3.31%) led Damodaran to explicitly reaffirm that "international diversification still belongs in the toolkit" ([Data Update 1 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-1-for-2026-push-and-pull-of.html), 2026-01; [Data Update 4 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html), 2026-02).
- **On tariffs/globalization:** Frames 2025's tariff shock (Liberation Day, April 2025) not as a one-off Trump policy but the culmination of a decade-plus backlash against globalization that started losing steam well before 2025, extending across both developed and emerging-market politics; Damodaran does not expect "this genie" to go back in the bottle ([Data Update 4 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html), 2026-02).
- **On the Damodaran Bot / AI and Damodaran's own workflow:** The 2026-01 post states that, on reflection, there is "no aspect" of the annual data-collection process that an AI bot couldn't do better and faster, and that the plan is to hand more of the mechanical process to it going forward, framing the author's own differential advantage as multi-disciplinary/generalist judgment rather than mechanical number-crunching ([Data Update 1 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-1-for-2026-push-and-pull-of.html), 2026-01).
- **On bitcoin:** Long "straddled the middle" (currency-without-a-central-bank vs. "millennial gold" collectible framing). The 2025 stress test (trust-deficit year with a weak dollar, Fed independence questioned) was, in Damodaran's framing, close to the ideal environment for bitcoin to prove either thesis — instead bitcoin ended the year down (-6.4% in USD, -17.4% in EUR) and moved with a much higher correlation to US equities (risk-on sentiment) than to gold/silver, updating the view toward "draws in risk-seekers, not the risk-averse" ([Data Update 3 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-3-for-2026-trust-deficit.html), 2026-01, referencing the July 2025 Microstrategy/bitcoin post).

## 7. Cases and examples

- **Uber cost-of-capital shortcut (referenced retrospectively, valuation done June 2014).** For a young, high-growth, hard-to-model company, Damodaran used the 90th-percentile US cost of capital (12%) at that time as Uber's discount rate rather than building a bespoke bottom-up number, freeing time to focus on the harder judgment calls (revenue growth, margin path) ([Data Update 6 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-6-for-2025-from-macro-to.html), 2025-02).
- **Canadian bank lending-mandate testimony (reported 2025-02).** Testified against a Canadian legislative proposal to force banks to lend less to fossil-fuel companies and more to green-energy firms. Data point: Canadian banks in the sample generated an aggregate 8.1% ROE, with two-thirds earning below their cost of equity already — forcing a shift from their best (fossil-fuel) credits to their worst (green-energy, largely financial basket cases, in Damodaran's view) risked pushing banks into distress, with costs falling mainly on depositors, not shareholders ([Data Update 7 for 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/data-update-7-for-2025-end-game-in.html), 2025-02).
- **2025 "Liberation Day" tariff shock and its market read-through.** In April 2025 the US announced broad, unexpectedly large tariffs; equities fell sharply worldwide before recovering over the following months. Lesson: the shock hit equities/currencies/gold, but 20- and 30-year US Treasury rates barely moved over the full year (the 10-year fell 39bp), and the CDS-spread and sovereign-rating channels showed only a brief blip — illustrating that a single "bad news" narrative doesn't move every market by the same amount or in the same direction ([Data Update 3 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-3-for-2026-trust-deficit.html), 2026-01).
- **US sovereign downgrade, Moody's, 2025-05-16.** Moody's cut the US from Aaa to Aa1 (after the close on Friday, May 16); markets absorbed it with only a brief CDS-spread blip, but it forced a permanent change in Damodaran's own ERP-computation process (see §5) ([Sovereign Ratings, Default Risk and Markets](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/06/sovereign-ratings-default-risk-and.html), 2025-06; [Data Update 3 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/01/data-update-3-for-2026-trust-deficit.html); [Data Update 4 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html)).
- **Google's 100-year bond issuance (Feb 2026).** Cited as evidence that bond markets remain willing to extend enormous maturity/slack to the largest, most creditworthy AI-investing tech firms — undercutting the case that these firms need private credit for speed/flexibility ([Data Update 7 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-7-for-2026-debt-and-taxes.html), 2026-02).
- **Meta/Blue Owl private-credit data-center financing (Oct 2025).** Used as Damodaran's go-to example of a large, credit-worthy borrower using private credit anyway — read as a sign the deal either carries below-market pricing or unusually lender-friendly terms no prudent capital-markets lender would accept, i.e., not a sustainable model at scale ([Data Update 7 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-7-for-2026-debt-and-taxes.html), 2026-02).
- **Costco vs. luxury retailers.** Costco's ~5% operating margin (sub-par) still creates more value than luxury retailers with 2–3x higher margins but far lower sales volume/turnover — illustrating that margin alone is a poor cross-sectional value indicator; volume/turnover and capital efficiency matter as much ([Data Update 5 for 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/01/data-update-5-for-2024-profitability.html), 2024-01).
- **Argentina/Venezuela sovereign-risk repricing (2025–26).** Both moved from "uninsurable" to merely "very costly to insure" on sovereign CDS in 2025, even though their formal sovereign ratings lagged the improvement — illustrating Damodaran's point that ratings agencies are slow to react relative to market-based CDS pricing ([Data Update 4 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html), 2026-02).
- **AI/software sell-off (early 2026).** Cites the software-sector sell-off (CNBC, Feb 2026) as markets front-running Damodaran's own thesis that AI will compress high-margin software economics before revenue/margin damage is realized in filed financials — "You live by the sword (expectations of growth and high margins), you die by it" ([Data Update 6 for 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-6-for-2026-in-search-of.html), 2026-02).
