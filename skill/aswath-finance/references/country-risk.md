## 1. Core frameworks and principles

**Risk comes from operations, not domicile.** "A company's risk does not come from where it is incorporated, but where it does business" ([January 2018 Data Update 5](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/01/january-2018-data-update-5-country-risk.html), 2018-01). Coca-Cola (US-incorporated) is exposed to wherever it sells; Royal Dutch Shell (UK-listed) is exposed to wherever it extracts oil/gas — often making it *more* emerging-market-exposed than actual emerging-market companies ([Country Risk Mid-year 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/08/country-risk-mid-year-2019-update.html), 2019-08). Nvidia is US-incorporated but exposed to China risk through its near-total dependence on TSMC ([2024 Data Update](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/07/country-risk-my-2024-data-update.html), 2024-07). A company cannot reduce its risk by delisting on a "risky" exchange and relisting on a safer one — a South African miner that moves its listing from Johannesburg to London is still exposed to South African risk ([Jan 2018 Data Update 5](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/01/january-2018-data-update-5-country-risk.html), 2018-01).

**Four drivers of country risk**, restated consistently 2015–2026: (1) *political structure* — democracy vs. authoritarian, continuous vs. discontinuous risk; (2) *corruption*, an implicit tax; (3) *exposure to violence* (war, terrorism, internal strife); (4) *strength of the legal system* protecting property rights ([Country Risk 2022 Mid-year](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/07/country-risk-2022-mid-year-update.html), 2022-07; [2026 Edition](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and.html), 2026-07). Later updates add three "external"/structural amplifiers largely outside a country's control: *commodity dependence* (small/niche economies can't diversify away from it), *stage in the country life cycle* (young, growing economies carry more risk regardless of governance quality), and *climate change exposure* ([2024 Data Update](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/07/country-risk-my-2024-data-update.html), 2024-07). These "amplifiers" are not new for 2024, though: Damodaran's original three-bucket taxonomy (economic/political/legal) already listed **life-cycle stage** and **economic concentration** (commodity/industry dependence, worse for small economies that must specialize) as economic-risk drivers, alongside violence, corruption, discontinuous-change risk, and property-rights strength under political/legal risk ([Pictures of Global Risk – Part I](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/07/groundhog-day-in-greece-hijinks-in.html), 2015-07).

**Judging risk-taking (including a government's) requires four separate lenses, not just outcome.** Outcome alone ("it worked out, so it was a good decision") is how most people — including governments trumpeting a bailout's profit — judge risk-taking, but a fuller assessment asks: (1) *outcome*; (2) *process* — did the decision make sense with the information available at the time, harder to assess but more honest; (3) *side costs and benefits* imposed on parties other than the risk-taker (a rescued company can make money for the government while still disadvantaging prudent competitors); (4) *effect on future risk-taking behavior* — rewarding a risk ex post teaches everyone to take more of that risk (the GM bailout's wrong lesson: "if you take risks, make sure that you are a big firm with the right connections"). Only when a risk-taker faces many repeated, similar bets with no material side effects is outcome-only judgment reasonable (a fund manager who beats the market ten years running) ([How do you evaluate risk taking?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2010/11/how-do-you-evaluate-risk-taking.html), 2010-11).

**Democracy vs. dictatorship is a trade-off in risk type, not a verdict.** Democracies create *continuous* risk — rules shift with each election/policy cycle. Dictatorships suppress day-to-day volatility but store up *discontinuous* risk — rare but wrenching, catastrophic changes ([A Risk Argument](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/09/risk-argument-democracies-versus.html), 2009-09; restated through [2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/07/country-risk-july-2023-update.html) and [2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/07/country-risk-my-2024-data-update.html) updates). Damodaran personally prefers democracy because market-traded instruments (options, futures) hedge continuous risk well, while insurance — the tool for discontinuous risk — is always imperfect ([2009-09](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/09/risk-argument-democracies-versus.html)).

**Country risk is not fully diversifiable.** The 1980s-era argument that global investors can diversify away country risk (so no premium is needed) breaks down because cross-market correlations have risen — a political crisis in São Paulo now drags down New York, London, Mumbai, Shanghai together ([Country Risk Midyear 2018](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/08/country-risk-midyear-update-for-2018.html), 2018-08). Two governing propositions Damodaran repeats: (1) if country risk is diversifiable and investors are globally diversified, ERP should be the same everywhere; since correlation has risen and many investors aren't fully global, ERP *should* vary by country; (2) exposure should be measured by where a business *operates*, not where it is incorporated (2018-08).

**Three-bucket model for bringing country risk into valuation** ([The Dark Side of Globalization](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/07/the-dark-side-of-globalization-update.html), 2017-07):
1. *Country-specific, continuous risk* (corruption, delay/inefficiency, everyday volatility) → build into **expected cash flows** (as an operating cost, a lag, or an insurance-cost proxy).
2. *Macro/systematic risk* (general country risk that markets price) → build into the **discount rate** via the equity risk premium.
3. *Discrete, catastrophic risk* (nationalization, terrorism, war-ending-the-business) → handle as a **post-valuation probability-weighted adjustment**, not through the discount rate or cash-flow haircuts.

**Currency is a measurement mechanism, never a value driver.** "Restating that temperature as 35 degrees celsius may make it seem cooler … but does not alter the reality" (of a 95°F day) ([Decoding Currency Risk](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/07/decoding-currency-risk-pictures-of.html), 2015-07). A well-run company has the same value in dollars, euros, yen, or rubles, *provided* you keep inflation assumptions internally consistent between cash flows and discount rate. If value changes across currencies, either (a) you made an inflation-consistency error, or (b) you smuggled in a currency view (e.g., assuming the real strengthens) — which should be traded directly via FX, not laundered through a company valuation (2015-07; reaffirmed [2018](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/01/january-2018-data-update-4-currency.html), [2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/07/country-risk-july-2023-update.html), [2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/07/country-risk-my-2024-data-update.html), [2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and.html)). By 2026 Damodaran states it flatly: currencies "don't determine country risk and... should not command risk premiums, on their own" (2026-07).

**Life-cycle logic applies to countries, not just companies.** Young, growing economies carry more risk almost regardless of governance quality, just as young companies do ([2024 Data Update](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/07/country-risk-my-2024-data-update.html), 2024-07; [2026 Edition](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and.html), 2026-07). You cannot value a Venezuelan, Indian, or Brazilian company without an implicit or explicit country narrative; US/European companies can sometimes be valued without dwelling on country risk evolution (2026-07).

## 2. Methods and procedures

**Estimating a country's equity risk premium (ERP)** — stable core method since the 1990s, refined over time:
1. Estimate the **implied ERP for the S&P 500** (Damodaran's monthly-updated mature-market premium).
2. Get the country's **sovereign default spread**, from either (a) a US-dollar-denominated sovereign bond spread over Treasuries, (b) the **sovereign CDS spread**, or (c) a **default spread implied by the local-currency sovereign rating** (Moody's/S&P/Fitch).
3. **Scale up** the default spread by the ratio of (emerging-market equity index volatility) ÷ (emerging-market government bond index volatility) — because equities are riskier than sovereign bonds. This scalar has drifted: 1.222 (July 2018), 1.42 (July 2023), 1.55 (July 2026) ([Country Risk Midyear 2018](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/08/country-risk-midyear-update-for-2018.html); [2023 Update](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/07/country-risk-july-2023-update.html); [2026 Edition](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and.html)).
4. Country risk premium = default spread × scalar. Country ERP = mature-market ERP + country risk premium.
   - Example (July 2023): India Baa3, default spread 2.35%, scalar 1.42, mature ERP 5.00% → India ERP = 5.00% + 2.35%(1.42) = **8.33%** ([2023 Update](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/07/country-risk-july-2023-update.html)).
   - Example (July 2025, post-Moody's-downgrade): India rupee risk-free rate = govt bond 6.32% − default spread 2.16% = 4.16%; India ERP 7.46% → expected return on average Indian equity in rupees = 4.16% + 7.46% = **11.62%** ([2025 Story Behind the Numbers](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/07/country-risk-2025-story-behind-numbers.html), 2025-07).
   - Turkey worked example, same post: US$ risk-free 3.97%, Turkey ERP 10.87% → expected return in US$ = 14.84%; converting to lira using PPP with 20% Turkish vs. 2.5% US inflation → expected return in lira = 1.1484×(1.20/1.025) − 1 = **34.45%**.
5. For unrated/frontier countries, use the PRS composite score to find rated countries with similar scores and borrow their ERP (used since at least 2013; still the fallback in 2026).

**Non-market country risk scores (PRS, Euromoney, World Bank, the Economist) are not comparable to each other or cardinally meaningful.** They are rankings, not ratios — a PRS score of 80 is not "twice as safe" as a score of 40 — and scoring direction isn't even standardized (PRS: higher = safer; some rival services: higher = riskier), so mixing sources without checking convention silently flips your risk read ([Pictures of Global Risk – Part I](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/07/groundhog-day-in-greece-hijinks-in.html), 2015-07; reaffirmed [May You Live in Exciting Times](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/07/may-you-live-in-times-updated-picture.html), 2016-07).

**Backing out a developed-vs-emerging differential ERP empirically from priced multiples**, as a cross-check on the default-spread-based country ERP: with firms assumed in stable growth, price-to-book = (ROE − g)/(Cost of equity − g), so Cost of equity = [(ROE − g)/(P/BV)] + g. Compute median P/BV and ROE separately for a "developed markets" bucket and an "emerging markets" bucket each year (same growth-rate assumption, e.g., g = the US T-bond rate, for both), solve for each group's implied cost of equity, and the gap is the market-priced differential ERP. Applied to 2004–2013 data, this differential fell from 2.5% and higher (2004–2006) to near zero by end-2012 before a partial 2013 rebound (Damodaran sees the norm as the post-2008 average of about 1–1.5%) — evidence that pricing convergence between developed and emerging markets was driven by a shrinking risk differential (and secondarily by developed-market multiples falling, not emerging-market multiples rising or a profitability shift, since emerging-market ROE stayed above developed-market ROE throughout) ([Developed versus Emerging Markets: Convergence or Divergence?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/07/developed-versus-emerging-markets.html), 2013-07).

**Estimating a currency's risk-free rate** ([Jan 2018 Data Update 4](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/01/january-2018-data-update-4-currency.html), 2018-01; refined [2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/06/sovereign-ratings-default-risk-and.html), [2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and.html)):
- **Method A (government-bond netting):** Risk-free rate in currency X = local-currency government bond rate − default spread implied by the sovereign's local-currency rating (or CDS). Necessary because local-currency sovereign default is real — of 58 sovereign defaults 1996–2012, 31 were local-currency (2018-01). Example: Nigeria Naira bond 14.12%, B2 rating default spread 5.64% → Naira risk-free rate = 8.48%.
- **Method B (differential inflation, preferred when bonds are untraded/manipulated):** Risk-free(currency) = (1+ risk-free rate in base currency)×(1+ expected inflation in currency)/(1+ expected inflation in base currency) − 1. Example: US risk-free 2.41%, US inflation 1.75%, India inflation 6% → India risk-free ≈ 6.66% (approx.) or 6.68% (precise). Applied to Venezuela gives a risk-free rate of ~3,814%, "a much more believable number given the hyper inflation," vs. a nonsensical <10% from the raw (manipulated) bond rate.
- **After the May 2025 Moody's downgrade of the US to Aa1**, Damodaran had to extend Method A to the US dollar itself: US$ risk-free rate = T-bond rate − default spread for Aa1 rating = 4.24% − 0.27% = **3.97%** on July 1, 2025 ([Country Risk 2025](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/07/country-risk-2025-story-behind-numbers.html), 2025-07; the [June post's](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/06/sovereign-ratings-default-risk-and.html) first cut was 4.41% − 0.40% = 4.01% on May 30). The "mature market premium" is now the implied S&P 500 ERP *net of* this US default spread (4.48% − 0.27% = 4.21% in mid-2025; 4.42% − 0.22% = 4.20% in mid-2026, per the [2026 Edition](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and.html)), and in 2025 it was applied only to the eleven countries still rated Aaa.

**Company/project hurdle rate assembly** (full stack, [2025 Story Behind the Numbers](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/07/country-risk-2025-story-behind-numbers.html), 2025-07; [2026 Edition](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and.html)):
1. Pick a currency of analysis and its risk-free rate (per above).
2. Determine the company's/project's **operating exposure weights** across countries — by revenue (best for consumer/service firms), by production/assets (best for natural-resource or asset-heavy firms), or a blend (manufacturing). Weighted-average ERP = Σ(country ERP × exposure weight).
3. Add a **relative risk measure** (beta or bottom-up/industry-average beta — CAPM-purity is explicitly not required here) to scale the ERP for the specific business: Cost of equity = risk-free rate + β × ERP.
4. For debt: cost of debt = risk-free rate + company default spread (+ country default spread if not already embedded, e.g., via a synthetic rating for an unrated emerging-market firm). A company in a country with sovereign default risk often carries a "double burden" — its own spread plus the sovereign's (2025-07).
5. Keep growth-rate/cash-flow inflation assumptions consistent with the currency and risk-free rate chosen (Step 5, currency consistency).

**Worked hurdle-rate example (Coca-Cola, July 2018):** with beta 0.80 and risk-free rate 3%, cost of equity for a Nigeria project = 3% + 0.80(13.15%) = 13.52%; Chile project = 3% + 0.80(6.22%) = 7.98%; a Canada/US project = 3% + 0.80(5.37%) = 7.30%. Using one blended global hurdle rate instead (e.g., 8.52% from a revenue-weighted ERP of 6.90%) systematically overinvests in the riskiest markets and underinvests in the safest ([Country Risk Midyear 2018](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/08/country-risk-midyear-update-for-2018.html)).

**Handling discrete/catastrophic risk (e.g., nationalization) — three options, only one recommended** ([Governments and Value: Part 1](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2012/04/governments-and-value-part-1.html), 2012-04):
- Option 1 (bump the discount rate) — rejected: discount rates are "blunt instruments" suited to volatility, not to survival/truncation risk.
- Option 2 (haircut near-term cash flows) — workable only for the first year or two; unworkable for later years.
- Option 3 (preferred): value the company normally to get a "going concern" value, then apply
  `Value = DCF value × (1 − P(nationalization)) + Liquidation/expropriation proceeds × P(nationalization)`.
  Worked example: Dominguez & Cia (Venezuelan packaging co.), DCF value 483M VEB, 20% nationalization probability, 50% of fair value recovered if nationalized → adjusted value = 483(0.8) + 483(0.5)(0.2) = 435M VEB; net of debt/cash, equity ≈ 212M VEB vs. traded 211M VEB (roughly fair).

**Handling government subsidies** — two methods, either defensible ([Governments and Value II](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2012/04/governments-and-value-ii-subsidies-and.html), 2012-04): (1) bake the subsidized inputs (cheap debt rate, tax holiday, price support) directly into the DCF and separately compute an unsubsidized "clean" value so you know how much of value is subsidy-dependent; or (2) value the company at unsubsidized terms first, then add the present value of the subsidy stream separately (only for the years you believe the subsidy will last). Worked example: Tesla with a $250M DOE loan at 3% vs. a 9% market rate — value with subsidized debt $10.40/share vs. unsubsidized $9.60/share; narrow subsidy value ≈ $0.80/share; expanding to broader subsidies (tax credits, revenue support) pushes value per share to $18.17.

**Handling corruption/bribery as a cost** — three options ([Governments and Value III](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2012/04/governments-and-value-iii-bribery.html), 2012-04): treat bribes as an explicit operating expense (rarely possible — usually unreported); treat corruption as an **implicit, unofficial tax** on income (Damodaran's preferred framing — PwC's 2001 "opacity index" estimated China's corruption-adjusted effective tax burden at 46%); or raise the cost of capital to compensate for "government partners" who take a cut of income without providing capital.

## 3. Rules of thumb and heuristics

- **Never blend currencies or inflation regimes in one analysis** — it's "akin to averaging the temperature in New York (measured in fahrenheit) with the temperature in Frankfurt (measured in celsius)" ([Jan 2018 Data Update 4](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/01/january-2018-data-update-4-currency.html)).
- **Pick a currency, stick with it, make inflation assumptions explicit** — moving to a "stable" currency (e.g., US dollars for Latin American analysis) doesn't remove inflation risk, it just relocates it from the discount rate to the exchange-rate forecast in your cash flows ([Decoding Currency Risk](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/07/decoding-currency-risk-pictures-of.html), 2015-07).
- **Use purchasing power parity (PPP) for expected exchange rates in valuation**, even knowing PPP deviates short-term — because it cancels out inflation-estimation error in both cash flows and discount rate simultaneously, and keeps your currency views from hijacking your company valuation (2015-07; reaffirmed 2024, 2026).
- **A government bond rate is a risk-free rate only if the sovereign is truly default-free (Aaa/AAA).** Using it uncleaned for any other rating double counts risk, especially if you're also using a high ERP. This is now true even for the **US dollar** post-May-2025 (2025-06).
- **There is no single corporate cost of capital for a multi-business, multi-geography company.** A firm in 5 businesses × 10 geographies has, in principle, 50 different costs of capital; using one blended number causes safe businesses/geographies to cross-subsidize risky ones ([2024 Data Update](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/07/country-risk-my-2024-data-update.html)).
- **When exposure straddles two countries (production in one, sales in another), ask where the risk actually bites** — production disruption risk vs. revenue-volatility risk — and weight accordingly, or blend if both matter ([2026 Edition](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and.html)).
- **Sovereign CDS spreads react in real time but overshoot; ratings are accurate on average but slow.** Use both — CDS to fill the gap when you distrust a stale rating, ratings as the anchor otherwise ([Wisdom and Madness of Crowds](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/11/the-wisdom-and-madness-of-crowds.html), 2024-11; recurring theme across annual updates).
- **"Go where it is darkest."** The biggest valuation payoffs are in situations with the most compounded uncertainty (company + country + currency + commodity risk stacked together), precisely because that's where the herd abandons first-principles valuation ([Go Where It Is Darkest](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/11/go-where-it-is-darkest-when-company.html), 2014-11).
- **Corruption test for whether a "strategic"/"China" argument is real**: for China specifically, the operative test is "preferred competitor" status — ask whether the Chinese government structurally favors your company (like Baidu over Google), not whether the market is large ([If it is a Strategic Growth Investment in China](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/03/if-it-is-strategic-growth-investment-in.html), 2014-03).
- **Political prediction/betting markets are more dynamic than polls but far more volatile**, and can be moved by a handful of large, biased trades (one trader put almost $30 million on a Trump win in 2024, pushing up the price) — treat them as one input, not oracle truth, and remember you can only *trade*, not *value*, an asset with no cash flows ([Wisdom and Madness of Crowds](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/11/the-wisdom-and-madness-of-crowds.html), 2024-11).

## 4. Myths and mistakes Damodaran calls out

- **Myth: "The government bond rate is always the risk-free rate in that currency."** False — governments frequently default even on local-currency debt rather than debase the currency; 31 of 58 sovereign defaults 1996–2012 were local-currency ([Jan 2018 Data Update 4](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/01/january-2018-data-update-4-currency.html)).
- **Myth: "There is a single global risk-free rate"** (e.g., "just use the lowest-yielding currency, like CHF or JPY, for everything"). Risk-free rates differ across currencies mainly because *expected inflation* differs — not because some currencies are inherently safer for discounting any cash flow ([2018-01](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/01/january-2018-data-update-4-currency.html)).
- **Myth: pegged exchange rates are permanently reliable.** If a pegged currency's inflation runs persistently above the currency it's pegged to, the peg will eventually break — the differential-inflation calculation is an early warning signal (explicitly warns analysts using the US T-bond rate for USD-pegged currencies, 2018-01).
- **Myth: switching the valuation currency (e.g., rubles to euros) changes value or reduces risk.** It doesn't, if you're internally consistent; if it appears to, you've smuggled in a currency forecast (2015-07, 2018-01, 2024-07, 2026-07 — repeated almost every year).
- **Myth: risk exposure is determined by country of incorporation/listing.** Rejected consistently 2018–2026; risk comes from where a company *operates* (produces and sells), and this reshapes both intrinsic valuation and relative-multiple comparisons.
- **Myth: currency risk deserves its own separate risk premium.** By 2026 Damodaran's firm position: currency risk is a *symptom* of country risk (unstable countries have unstable currencies), already embedded in the equity risk premium; adding a further currency premium double-counts, and much currency risk is diversifiable for global-portfolio investors anyway ([2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/07/country-risk-july-2023-update.html), [2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and.html)).
- **Myth: hedging is generically good corporate practice.** Damodaran's "three-bucket" risk-management test (developed pre-2008, restated 2008-11/2008-11): (1) risk that should pass through to investors (e.g., a commodity company hedging away the commodity exposure investors bought it *for* — undercuts the investment thesis); (2) risk that should be hedged (input costs threatening survival/operating efficiency, e.g., an airline hedging jet fuel); (3) risk that should be sought out and exploited (where the firm has genuine competitive advantage). Applying this: Southwest's long-standing, consistent fuel hedging makes sense (bucket 2); airlines that only started hedging *after* oil spiked were market-timing, not risk-managing; Aracruz (a pulp/paper company) making a currency speculation bet was "plain dumb" regardless of outcome — never judge a hedging decision by whether it made money, judge the process ([Corporate Hedging: The Down Side](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2008/11/corporate-hedging-down-side.html) and [Answers to Questions](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2008/11/corporate-hedging-answers-to-questions.html), 2008-11).
- **Myth: ratings agencies are corrupt/biased due to issuer-pays conflicts.** Damodaran downplays this for sovereigns specifically (fees are "paltry," so the conflict-of-interest story is "overdone"); the *real* problem with agencies is that they are slow to react and carry regional biases (favoring rich countries with default-free histories, penalizing Latin America for its history) ([2023 Update](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/07/country-risk-july-2023-update.html)).
- **Myth: "buzzword" arguments (optionality, growth potential, "strategic," disruptive, China) substitute for analysis.** All five require converting the story into explicit numbers (market size, exclusivity, excess returns, competitive advantage) or they're empty rationalizations for overpaying — and Damodaran finds a *negative* correlation between how often an analyst invokes these words and the quality of the underlying research ([If it is a Strategic Growth Investment in China](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/03/if-it-is-strategic-growth-investment-in.html), 2014-03).
- **Error owned and corrected (2026):** an earlier July 2026 version of the country-ERP table used incomplete sovereign default-spread data; Damodaran republished a corrected (lower) version and publicly thanked readers who caught the error — illustrating that even the flagship dataset is not gospel ([2026 Edition](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and.html)).

## 5. How Damodaran's views evolved

- **2009–2010 (financial crisis as turning point):** Pre-crisis, Damodaran treated developed-market sovereigns as unquestionably default-free and country risk as an emerging-market-only concern. The Greek sovereign downgrades (2009-12) and the 2010 Greek/EU crisis forced the explicit question "[What if nothing is risk-free?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2010/07/what-if-nothing-is-risk-free.html)" (2010-07) — a rupture with the textbook assumption that US/UK/German government bonds are automatically risk-free.
- **2009–2010, convergence thesis:** Damodaran documents developed and emerging markets converging on nearly every historical dividing line — financial-market depth, liquidity/capital-access crises, government default risk, government intrusiveness, and currency trust — arguing the 2008 crisis exposed developed markets adopting "the worst traits" of emerging markets while emerging markets matured ([Emerging vs. Developed Markets](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2010/01/emerging-versus-developed-markets.html), 2010-01).
- **2012:** First systematic treatment of *government as a direct driver of company value* (not just via tax code) — the three-part "Governments and Value" series (nationalization, subsidies, corruption), prompted by Argentina's YPF nationalization.
- **2013–2015:** Formalizes the country-ERP methodology (default spread × equity/bond volatility scalar) and starts the twice-yearly (January/July) country-risk update ritual that continues through 2026. The four-part "Pictures of Global Risk" series (July 2015) is the first comprehensive synthesis of measurement, valuation treatment, and currency treatment together.
- **2016–2017:** Political shocks (Brexit, Trump election) push Damodaran to treat *political events themselves* as market-moving country-risk data points, and to formalize the three-bucket cash-flow/discount-rate/post-valuation adjustment framework for country risk ([Dark Side of Globalization](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/07/the-dark-side-of-globalization-update.html), 2017-07).
- **2018:** Devotes a dedicated post to currency mechanics (misconceptions 1–3) after years of it being a subsection — signals currency confusion as a distinct, recurring analyst failure mode worth isolating.
- **2021–2022:** China tech crackdown (2021-09) and the Russia-Ukraine war (2022-03) become case studies for *discrete political risk hitting reality in real time* — Damodaran's valuations of Alibaba/Tencent/JD/Didi explicitly price in "government as constraint" scenarios; the Russia war post is the most granular real-time market-reaction dataset (bond, equity, commodity, safe-haven, sector-by-sector).
- **2023:** Explicitly reframes the India-vs-China "which is the better emerging market bet" debate as continuous-democratic-chaos (India) vs. discontinuous-authoritarian-control (China) risk — and for the first time suggests the battleground is "shifting in favor of India," while cautioning it's "too early to make a judgment" ([2023 Update](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/07/country-risk-july-2023-update.html)).
- **2024:** Adds *climate change exposure* and *country life-cycle stage* as new formal country-risk drivers, alongside commodity dependence; and for the first time, India's sovereign CDS (0.83%) trades tighter than China's (1.05%), which Damodaran flags as historically new ([2024 Data Update](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/07/country-risk-my-2024-data-update.html)).
- **2025 (methodological rupture):** Moody's downgrade of the US from Aaa to Aa1 (May 16, 2025) breaks the long-standing convenience of treating the US Treasury rate as automatically risk-free and the S&P 500 ERP as automatically the "mature market premium." Damodaran rebuilds the entire global ERP scaffolding around a US-default-spread-adjusted risk-free rate and a shrunk set of ~11 truly Aaa countries, which now get a *lower* ERP than the US ([Sovereign Ratings, Default Risk and Markets](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/06/sovereign-ratings-default-risk-and.html), 2025-06). Also broadens the political lens beyond emerging markets — "Investing Politics" (2025-03) treats US tariff policy and the globalization backlash as first-order valuation inputs for the first time at this scale.
- **2026:** Treats a live Middle East war (Iran) and oil shock as a real-time test of market-narrative-reading methodology — extracting the market's view from oil spot vs. futures prices, Treasury yields and default spreads rather than from expert forecasts ([Oil, War and the Global Economy](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/04/oil-war-and-global-economy-markets.html), 2026-04).

## 7. Cases and examples

- **Tata Group and Adris Grupa, emerging-market conglomerate structure (2010):** Valuing Tata Chemicals, Tata Steel, and Tata Motors, a third to half of estimated value came from cross-holdings in other Tata companies (25–30 of them) — a structure with a benign historical root: in decades when Indian capital markets lacked information/research infrastructure, a trusted family name substituted for due diligence, and an internal family "capital market" reallocated cash across group companies. Today the same structure creates a valuation problem (valuing one company requires valuing dozens, and some cross-held entities are private and unvaluable except at book), which can make the market value family-group companies *below* their sum-of-parts, an emerging-market-governance-linked discount distinct from country ERP. Separately, Adris Grupa (Croatian tobacco), with cash >20% of firm value against below-cost-of-capital returns on operating assets, illustrates that markets in weaker-governance environments can price a currency unit of cash at less than its face value when the accumulating company has a poor investment track record — unlike Apple, trusted to redeploy cash well ([Cash and Cross holdings](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2010/05/cash-and-cross-holdings.html), 2010-05).
- **Greece (2009–2010):** Pre-Euro, Greece was "more an 'emerging' than a 'developed' market" (BBB- rating, 1995); Euro adoption in 2002 improved its credit standing partly on fiscal discipline, partly on implicit EU backing. The December 2009 downgrades (S&P A- → BBB+, plus a Moody's downgrade) and subsequent default drama tested whether that implicit guarantee was real — lesson: implicit sovereign backstops carry real economic content until tested ([Greece, EU and Implicit Backing](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/12/greece-eu-and-more-on-implicit-backing.html), 2009-12).
- **Argentina/YPF (2012):** Nationalization of YPF (majority owned by Spain's Repsol) triggered a 21% drop in YPF shares in New York trading (already down ~50% for the year) — lesson: in countries like Argentina, Venezuela or Russia, the possibility of government capriciousness has to be priced into what you pay, via the probability-weighted approach above ([Governments and Value: Part 1](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2012/04/governments-and-value-part-1.html), 2012-04).
- **Vale & Lukoil (Nov 2014):** Case study in "go where it is darkest" — company (weak governance, dual-class shares, government golden share), country (Brazil/Russia CDS spikes), currency (Real/Ruble collapse), and commodity (iron ore, oil) risk all hit simultaneously. Damodaran's DCFs — Vale with an 8.25% ERP (weighted by reserve geography), Lukoil with an updated 9.50% Russia ERP and depressed base-year operating income — valued Vale at $19.40 vs. an $8.53 market price, and Lukoil at $50.56 vs. $45.30 — Damodaran already owned Vale (and planned to add) and planned to buy Lukoil, explicitly accepting "no power to exert change" corporate-governance risk at the price ([Go Where It Is Darkest](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/11/go-where-it-is-darkest-when-company.html), 2014-11).
- **Oil price collapse (2014):** Documents a "clueless trifecta" — forecasters (consensus $105/barrel for 2014), oil companies (Chevron budgeted at $110, calling "$100 the new $20"), and investors (institutional money still flowing in through Q3) all missed the 40%+ price drop. Damage was concentrated in high-cost, highly-levered producers and heavily oil-exporting sovereigns (Russia CDS +137.83%, Venezuela CDS more than tripled, from Sept 16, 2014) ([Oil Price Shock](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/12/the-oil-price-shock-primary-secondary.html), 2014-12).
- **China equity market (Aug 2015):** Despite a 32% crash from its June peak, Chinese stocks remained the most expensive market in the world on PE/EV-EBITDA versus BRIC peers and developed markets, due to a market structure (multi-class shares, VIEs, capital controls, "morality play" government interventions) that suppresses long-term investors in favor of momentum traders — Damodaran's forecast: slower real GDP growth (closer to 5% a year), continued "pricing not investing," and buyer-beware ([Beijing Blunders](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/beijing-blunders-bull-in-china-shop.html), 2015-08).
- **Brexit → Trump parallel (2016):** Damodaran republished the June 2016 Brexit post with words swapped to "Trump," showing near-identical market and behavioral dynamics — betting markets lagging and then snapping late, "narrative beats numbers," and experts collectively ignored — arguing both were evidence that markets are "biased counting machines" susceptible to large, motivated traders, not pure aggregators of information ([Trump Effect on Markets](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/11/the-trump-effect-on-markets-financial.html), 2016-11).
- **China tech crackdown (2021):** Alibaba, Tencent, JD.com, Didi repriced (market cap down 46%, 17%, 7% respectively over the year; Didi IPO down 42% from offer) as investors recognized Beijing's crackdown was "about Control, not Consumers or Competition." Damodaran's DCF-based re-valuation still found Alibaba (12.7% undervalued) and Tencent (8%) cheap even after adjusting for a more adversarial government, with Didi carrying the most two-sided (upside/downside) government-dependency risk ([China's Tech Crackdown](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/09/chinas-tech-crackdown-market-adjustment.html), 2021-09).
- **Russia invades Ukraine (2022):** Real-time multi-asset case study — sovereign CDS, corporate bond spreads, equity indices, sector rotation (only energy positive), commodity spikes (nickel trading suspended after a ~$100k/ton move), gold vs. Bitcoin as safe havens (gold held up, Bitcoin gave back gains), and a stated worst-case tail risk (nuclear/chemical escalation) that "no investing strategy... will protect you from" ([Russia in Ukraine](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/03/russia-and-ukraine-let-loose-dogs-of-war.html), 2022-03).
- **1MDB (Malaysia):** Cited as the cautionary opposite of Norges — a sovereign wealth fund captured by politically-connected insiders, used to fund Hollywood films and personal lifestyles, ending in the jailing of PM Najib Razak and a $3.9 billion charge against Goldman Sachs; contrasted with Norges' transparent, marginally-outperforming, professionally governed model ([Sugar Daddy Effect](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/10/the-sugar-daddy-effect-corporate.html), 2024-10).
- **Moody's US downgrade (May 2025):** Downgraded Aaa→Aa1; markets barely reacted (small equity gain the following Monday, yields normalized within two weeks) because (a) S&P (2011) and Fitch (2023) had already downgraded, removing surprise; (b) government borrowing is dwarfed by private capital flows; (c) the Aaa/Aa1 default-spread gap is tiny (Austria/Finland at Aa1 traded only ~0.15–0.20% over Aaa Germany). Damodaran treats it as more symbolic than financial — the end of "US exceptionalism" in ratings treatment — but reworks the entire global ERP scaffold in response ([Sovereign Ratings...Downgrade](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/06/sovereign-ratings-default-risk-and.html), 2025-06).
- **Iran war / oil shock (March 2026):** Live application of Damodaran's "read the market narrative" method mid-crisis — divergence between Brent (+49.9%) and WTI (+48.6%) traced to the Strait of Hormuz chokepoint vs. US production insulation; long bond yields rose more than short bills, read as the market pricing "persistent" (not transitory) inflation, while oil futures rising less than spot signaled supply interruptions seen as more temporary than permanent ([Oil, War and the Global Economy](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/04/oil-war-and-global-economy-markets.html), 2026-04).
