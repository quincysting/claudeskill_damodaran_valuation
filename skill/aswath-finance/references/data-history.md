## 1. Core frameworks and principles

**Why the annual ritual exists.** Since the early 1990s Damodaran has spent the first 1–2 weeks of each year downloading raw data on every publicly traded firm globally and converting it into industry/country averages, for four selfish reasons stated explicitly: gain perspective on what's normal, use averages as a mean-reversion anchor, counter made-up claims ("US companies pay too little/too much in tax"), and stress-test rules of thumb ([Data Update 2013](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/01/data-update-2013-dark-side-of-numbers.html), 2013-01; [Data Update 1 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/01/data-update-1-for-2020-setting-table.html), 2020-01; [Data Update 1 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/01/data-update-1-for-2023-year-that-was.html), 2023-01).

**Implied equity risk premium (ERP) is Damodaran's central instrument.** Rather than use historical stock-minus-bond returns (backward-looking, huge standard errors — 2.1% on the 4.84% 1928–2020 estimate, so the true ERP could be anywhere from 0.64% to 9.04%; 2.15% for 1928–2022, a ±2-standard-error range of 2.34%–10.94%), Damodaran backs out a market-implied ERP the way you'd compute a bond's yield to maturity: take the index level as "price," project dividends+buybacks (using trailing cash flow, consensus analyst earnings forecasts for the near years, then converging to a terminal growth rate set equal to the risk-free rate), and solve for the discount rate; ERP = that IRR minus the risk-free rate ([Jan 2016 Data Update 1](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-1-us-equity.html), 2016-01; [Data Update 2 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/01/data-update-2-for-2021-price-of-risk.html), 2021-01; [Data Update 2 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/01/data-update-2-for-2023-rocky-year-for.html), 2023-01). This is "model agnostic" — it needs no belief in CAPM, only a price and a cash-flow forecast ([Data Update 2 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/01/data-update-2-for-2022-us-stocks-kept.html), 2022-01).

**Country risk comes from operations, not incorporation or listing.** A company's risk exposure is set by *where it does business* — Coca-Cola and Nestlé have material emerging-market exposure despite US/Swiss incorporation; Embraer (Brazil) and Infosys (India) are global businesses that happen to be incorporated in emerging markets ([Jan 2016 Update 3](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-3-country-risk.html), 2016-01; [Data Update 4 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/data-update-4-country-risk-and-currency.html), 2020-02; [Data Update 4 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/02/data-update-4-for-2023-country-risk.html), 2023-02). Weight a company's country ERP by revenue exposure for asset-light multinationals (Coca-Cola) or production exposure for resource extractors (Aramco = entirely Saudi; Royal Dutch weighted across production geographies) ([Data Update 4 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/data-update-4-country-risk-and-currency.html), 2020-02).

**Currency is a measurement device, not a risk factor — valuations must be currency-invariant.** If your DCF is internally consistent about inflation, valuing the same company in USD, EUR, or INR should give the same answer, because higher local risk-free rates (from higher inflation) are exactly offset by higher nominal growth. Violating this ("casual dollarization": USD discount rate but local-currency cash flows without currency conversion) is one of the most common valuation errors Damodaran flags ([Jan 2017 Update 3](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-3-cracking.html), 2017-01; [Data Update 3 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/data-update-3-for-2020-price-of-risk.html), 2020-02).

**Cost of capital is the "Swiss Army knife of finance"** — simultaneously the corporate hurdle rate (investment principle), the capital-structure optimizer (financing principle), the divining rod for cash return (dividend principle), and the DCF discount rate ([Jan 2016 Update 4](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-4-costs-of.html), 2016-01; [Jan 2017 Update 6](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-6-cost-of.html), 2017-01). Because it is used everywhere, it is also the most abused: analysts turn it into a "dumping ground" for every fear (illiquidity premiums, small-cap premiums, subjective risk add-ons) instead of a disciplined risk-adjusted number.

**The real debt trade-off vs. the illusory one.** Real benefits of debt: the tax shield (interest is deductible, equity cash flows are not) and a secondary discipline effect (forced payments deter bad investment by mature, cash-rich firms). Real costs: expected bankruptcy costs (probability × cost of distress) and agency costs between lenders and equity holders. Illusory arguments on both sides: "debt raises ROE" and "debt is cheaper than equity" ignore that cost of equity rises to offset; "debt lowers net income" ignores the smaller equity base it's earned on ([Jan 2016 Update 6](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-6-debt-double.html), 2016-01; [Jan 2017 Update 8](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/02/january-2017-data-update-8-dark-and.html), 2017-02; [Data Update 6 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/02/data-update-6-for-2023-wake-up-call-for.html), 2023-02).

**Dividends are residual, not primary, cash flows.** The "correct" sequence is invest → finance → pay out whatever's left (FCFE = free cash flow to equity = net income + D&A − capex − Δworking capital − net debt repayment). In practice, inertia and me-tooism invert this: firms decide the dividend first and then distort investment/financing to fund it — "dysfunctional dividends" ([Jan 2016 Update 7](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-7-dividends.html), 2016-01; [Jan 2017 Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/02/january-2017-data-update-9-dividends.html), 2017-02; [Data Update 9 for 2018](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/02/january-2018-data-update-9-dividends.html), 2018-02; [Data Update 7 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/03/data-update-7-for-2023-dividends.html), 2023-03).

**Excess returns, not accounting profit, define a "good" business.** Compare ROIC to cost of capital (or ROE to cost of equity for financials): value creators earn ≥2% above cost of capital, value destroyers ≥2% below, the rest (within 2%) are "value zeros." Growth in a business earning below its cost of capital destroys value — a recurring, decade-spanning finding ([Jan 2016 Update 5](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-5-making-case.html), 2016-01; [Jan 2017 Update 7](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-7.html), 2017-01; [Data Update 5 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/02/data-update-5-for-2023-earnings-test.html), 2023-02).

**Risk is danger plus opportunity, not just downside.** Damodaran anchors on the Chinese symbol-pair for "crisis" as danger + opportunity: opportunity without danger is a Ponzi-scheme fantasy, danger without expected compensating opportunity is foolhardy, and risk is fundamentally about *uncertainty of outcomes*, not expected bad outcomes (a company you expect to stay badly run is not risky on that account — you've already priced it in) ([Data Update 4 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/02/data-update-4-for-2022-risk-danger.html), 2022-02; [Data Update 3 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/data-update-3-for-2020-price-of-risk.html), 2020-02).

**Risk measurement has three families, pick what fits your philosophy:** price-based (high-low range, standard deviation, beta/covariance — for diversified traders), earnings-based (money-losing/making, coefficient of variation in earnings — for value investors who distrust prices), and proxies (market cap, PE class, industry — weak and easily debunked by data) ([Data Update 4 for 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/01/january-2019-data-update-4-many-faces.html), 2019-01; [Data Update 5 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/data-update-5-relative-risk-and-hurdle.html), 2020-02).

**Investments split into four types, and only one can be valued.** Assets (contractual/residual/conditional cash flows — stocks, bonds, real businesses) can be valued via DCF. Commodities, currencies, and collectibles can only be *priced* (demand/supply, storability, scarcity/desirability) — you can trade them but not value them; bitcoin is a currency (a poor one) or a collectible, never an asset ([Data Update 3 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/01/data-update-3-for-2021-currencies.html), 2021-01).

**The corporate life cycle governs everything** — risk exposure (young firms face more economic/micro/discrete risk), margins/ROIC (rise then fall), and dividend capacity (negative FCFE when young, cash accumulation early maturity, payout acceleration late maturity, liquidation-like payout in decline). Damodaran repeatedly tests this against the data and finds support ([Data Update 4 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/02/data-update-4-for-2022-risk-danger.html), 2022-02; [Data Update 5 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/02/data-update-5-for-2022-bottom-line.html), 2022-02; [Data Update 7 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/03/data-update-7-for-2023-dividends.html), 2023-03).

## 2. Methods and procedures

**Sample and classification.** All publicly traded firms with market cap > 0, worldwide (~42,700 in 2017 growing to ~47,900 by 2023), spanning ~135–150 countries. Classified into 5 broad geographic groups (US, Developed Europe, Emerging Markets [with India/China broken out], Japan, Australia/NZ/Canada) and ~94–96 industry groups built for time-series comparability, using S&P sector codes as a starting point but Damodaran's own finer breakdown ([Jan 2017 Update 1](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-1-promise-and.html), 2017-01; [Jan 2018 Update 1](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/01/january-2018-data-update-1-numbers-dont.html), 2018-01; [Data Update 1 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/01/data-update-1-for-2020-setting-table.html), 2020-01; [Data Update 1 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/01/data-update-1-for-2022-it-is-moneyball.html), 2022-01; [Data Update 1 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/01/data-update-1-for-2023-year-that-was.html), 2023-01).

**Timing convention:** market data (prices, rates, ERPs) as of Jan 1; accounting data as the trailing twelve months from the most recent filing (usually through ~Sept 30 of the prior year for calendar-year firms). Damodaran treats this as consistent, not as a mismatch, because it reflects the most current information a real investor would have ([Jan 2018 Update 1](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/01/january-2018-data-update-1-numbers-dont.html), 2018-01).

**Two standing accounting adjustments, applied to every company:** (1) capitalize R&D — treat it as a capital expense, not an operating expense, adjusting operating income and invested capital; (2) treat all lease commitments as debt, discounted at the pre-tax cost of debt — a practice followed for ~20 years before GAAP/IFRS partially caught up in 2019 (and even then, Damodaran's own PV-of-commitments lease-debt estimate for non-financials ran ~60% higher than the new accounting numbers) ([Jan 2019 Update 3](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/01/january-2018-data-update-3-playing.html), 2019-01; [Data Update 7 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/03/data-update-7-for-2020-debt-delusions.html), 2020-03).

**Industry-average aggregation, not simple average.** For ratios like PE, Damodaran sums the numerator and denominator across *all* firms in a group (including money-losers) and divides, rather than averaging individual ratios — this avoids both outlier distortion and the survivorship bias from dropping negative-earnings firms. Both an "aggregated across all firms" and an "aggregated across money-makers only" version are published so users can see the spread (e.g., broadcasting PE ranged 7.05–24.99 depending on method) ([Data Update 1 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/01/data-update-1-for-2022-it-is-moneyball.html), 2022-01).

**Beta estimation:** never trust a single company's regression beta (noisy, unstable, especially distorted around M&A/distress); use the average *unlevered* regression beta for the sector, then relever it to the company's own D/E ([Jan 2016 Update 4](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-4-costs-of.html), 2016-01; [Data Update 4 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/02/data-update-4-for-2022-risk-danger.html), 2022-02).

**Country equity risk premium build:**
1. Start with the implied US/mature-market ERP (e.g., 5.69% Jan 2017, 4.24% Jan 2022, 5.94% Jan 2023) as the base for all Aaa-rated ("mature") markets.
2. Get the country's sovereign rating (Moody's/S&P) or, absent one, its sovereign CDS spread or PRS political-risk score (used as a last resort via a lookup table against rated countries with similar scores) → this gives a *default spread*.
3. Scale the default spread up by a ratio of (emerging-market equity index volatility)/(emerging-market government-bond index volatility) — Damodaran has used ~1.16–1.34 historically — because equities are riskier than sovereign bonds.
4. Country ERP = Mature-market ERP + (Default Spread × Relative Volatility Ratio). Example, India Jan 2016: 6% (6.12% rounded down) + 2.44%×1.34 = 9.28%; India Jan 2022: 4.24% + 1.87%×1.16 = 6.42% ([Jan 2016 Update 3](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-3-country-risk.html), 2016-01; [Jan 2017 Update 4](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-4-country-risk.html), 2017-01; [Data Update 4 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/02/data-update-4-for-2022-risk-danger.html), 2022-02; [Data Update 4 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/02/data-update-4-for-2023-country-risk.html), 2023-02).

**Currency risk-free rate, four approaches, in order of preference/availability:**
1. **Government bond rate** directly — valid only if you believe the issuer is truly default-free (US, Germany, Switzerland, a handful of Aaa sovereigns).
2. **Government bond rate net of default spread** — Riskfree(local) = Govt bond rate − default spread based on the local-currency sovereign rating. (India Jan 2020: 6.56% − 1.59% = 4.95%.)
3. **Differential inflation / synthetic rate** — Riskfree(local) ≈ US$ riskfree + (local expected inflation − US expected inflation), with a compounding-correct version: (1+US rate)×(1+local inflation)/(1+US inflation) − 1.
4. **Intrinsic/synthetic from scratch** — riskfree = expected real growth (proxy for real rate) + expected inflation, useful when no usable government bond exists ([Jan 2017 Update 3](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-3-cracking.html), 2017-01; [Data Update 4 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/data-update-4-country-risk-and-currency.html), 2020-02).

**Intrinsic (fundamental) risk-free rate check for the US Treasury:** Intrinsic 10-yr rate = actual inflation rate + real GDP growth rate that year. Damodaran uses the gap between this and the actual T-bond rate as the central argument that the Fed does not set long-term rates — it follows them, at most nudging at the margin ([Jan 2016 Update 2](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-2-interest.html), 2016-01; [Jan 2017 Update 2](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-2-resilience.html), 2017-01; [Data Update 3 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/01/data-update-3-for-2023-inflation-and.html), 2023-01).

**Cost of capital build (per company, in US$ for comparability, then convert via differential inflation):** Cost of equity = Riskfree + Beta(unlevered sector, relevered) × Company/geography-weighted ERP. Cost of debt = Riskfree + Default spread (from actual bond rating, or a "synthetic rating" from interest-coverage/debt ratios for unrated firms), then after-tax at the marginal (not effective) tax rate of the country of incorporation (use the *highest* marginal rate across operating countries for true multinationals, since debt gets routed there — Apple example). Weights = market value of equity and debt (with lease PV added to debt) ([Jan 2016 Update 4](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-4-costs-of.html), 2016-01; [Jan 2017 Update 6](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-6-cost-of.html), 2017-01; [Data Update 4 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/02/data-update-4-for-2022-risk-danger.html), 2022-02).

**Debt definition, two-part test:** (1) does it create a contractual commitment you must meet in good times and bad? (2) does failure trigger business-ending consequences or loss of control to lenders? All interest-bearing debt and all lease commitments pass; accounts payable/supplier credit generally does not (no explicit interest) ([Data Update 7 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/03/data-update-7-for-2020-debt-delusions.html), 2020-03; [Data Update 6 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/02/data-update-6-for-2023-wake-up-call-for.html), 2023-02).

**Optimal capital structure**, mechanically: relever beta and default spread at each hypothetical debt ratio, recompute cost of capital, find the minimum — a genuine optimizer, not a fixed target. Optimal ratio rises with (a) the marginal tax rate, (b) EBITDA/enterprise-value (cash-generating capacity), and falls with business risk. Worked example: Adani Enterprises' actual debt (₹413.4bn) was more than double its estimated optimal (₹185.3bn) in 2023; the whole Adani Group carried ~3× its optimal debt in aggregate ([Data Update 6 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/02/data-update-6-for-2023-wake-up-call-for.html), 2023-02).

**ROIC/EVA computation:** After-tax operating income (with R&D and lease adjustments) ÷ invested capital (book debt + book equity − cash) at the *start* of the period; compare to cost of capital for the excess-return spread. Damodaran also computes a 10-year average ROIC to reduce single-year noise, noting the two approaches diverge a lot (single-year, 2016 trailing: ~63% of firms were value destroyers, ROIC 2%+ below cost of capital; 10-year average, firms with 10+ years of data: ~23% below cost of capital) ([Jan 2017 Update 7](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-7.html), 2017-01).

**Daily ERP tracking during a crisis month.** When a month turns volatile, Damodaran recomputes the S&P 500 implied ERP at the end of each trading day (holding cash-flow and growth assumptions fixed) to show that "the price of risk increases during a crisis" in real time, not just at the next annual update — first done for January 2016's China/oil-driven selloff (ERP rose ~0.60% on both the trailing and payout-adjusted measures, peaking January 20 before partly receding) and repeated for the 2020 COVID crash ([Jan 2016 Data Update 8](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/02/january-2016-data-update-8-pricing-with.html), 2016-02; [Data Update 2 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/01/data-update-2-for-2021-price-of-risk.html), 2021-01).

**Valuing the index (S&P 500) as a DIY template**, repeated each January: use consensus analyst EPS for years 1–2, taper growth to the risk-free rate over the next 3–5 years as the terminal-growth assumption; set payout ratio to trailing cash-return-to-earnings initially, moving to a sustainable payout (1 − g/ROE) in steady state; assume a risk-free-rate path; assume a "fair" ERP (usually a blend of long-run and post-2008 average); solve for index value and compare to actual level. Damodaran publishes the spreadsheet every year and explicitly invites readers to substitute their own assumptions rather than accept the published ones ([Data Update 2 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/01/data-update-2-for-2021-price-of-risk.html), 2021-01; [Data Update 2 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/01/data-update-2-for-2022-us-stocks-kept.html), 2022-01; [Data Update 2 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/01/data-update-2-for-2023-rocky-year-for.html), 2023-01).

## 3. Rules of thumb and heuristics

- **Skip fine-tuning the discount rate; spend the saved time on cash flows.** When in a hurry, use the distributional cost-of-capital percentiles directly: e.g., Jan 2017 — 8.03% median for a global firm, with 90% of global firms between 5.63% and 10.68%; Jan 2020 — 9.15% (US 90th percentile) for a young money-losing US firm like Casper, 6.02% (European 25th percentile) for a stable firm like Heineken; Jan 2021 — 4.16%/5.30%/5.73% for safe/average/risky firms given near-record-low rates. Mistakes in valuations "almost never" come from the discount rate; they come from growth/margin/cash-flow errors ([Jan 2017 Update 6](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-6-cost-of.html), 2017-01; [Data Update 5 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/data-update-5-relative-risk-and-hurdle.html), 2020-02; [Data Update 4 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/02/data-update-4-for-2021-hurdle-rate.html), 2021-02).
- **Diversification benefit is front-loaded:** ~80% of the achievable variance reduction from diversification arrives by about a dozen uncorrelated-ish stocks; concentrated vs. diversified is a legitimate choice depending on how confident you are in your specific thesis, not a right/wrong debate ([Data Update 4 for 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/01/january-2019-data-update-4-many-faces.html), 2019-01).
- **Forecast exchange rates via purchasing power parity (differential inflation), never via a house view.** It's the cheapest, most internally consistent approach, and idiosyncratic FX views should not be smuggled into a company valuation — if you have a currency view, trade the currency directly, don't distort the equity call ([Jan 2016 Update 2](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-2-interest.html), 2016-01; [Jan 2017 Update 3](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-3-cracking.html), 2017-01).
- **Replace absolute pricing rules of thumb ("cheap below book," "cheap below 6x EBITDA") with relative/percentile screens** computed fresh each year, since "cheap" is market- and country-specific ([Jan 2017 Update 10](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/03/january-2017-data-update-10-pricing-game.html), 2017-03).
- **Never use a single corporate-wide hurdle rate across multiple businesses or geographies** — use project/business/country-specific opportunity costs; a single corporate WACC lets safe divisions subsidize risky ones ("risk shifting") ([Data Update 4 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/02/data-update-4-for-2021-hurdle-rate.html), 2021-02).
- **Use the highest marginal tax rate across a multinational's operating countries** when computing the after-tax cost of debt, since debt gets routed to the highest-tax jurisdiction (Apple: ~21% effective tax but ~40% marginal in the US, which is where nearly all its debt sits) ([Jan 2017 Update 5](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-5-taxing-year.html), 2017-01).
- **Don't extrapolate "good business" rankings into stock picks** — the correlation between business quality (excess returns) and forward investment returns is weak-to-negative if the market has already priced in the quality gap; the worst-run businesses can be the best investments if sufficiently mispriced, and vice versa ([Jan 2017 Update 7](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-7.html), 2017-01).
- **Treat the yield-curve slope as weak evidence at best** for recession timing — Damodaran explicitly doubts it has much predictive power, even while acknowledging market obsession with it ([Data Update 3 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/01/data-update-3-inflation-and-its-ripple.html), 2022-01).
- **Don't extrapolate market lessons from a single volatile month.** After January 2016's selloff Damodaran cautioned against dredging up "temporal anomalies" like the January effect or the Super Bowl indicator to explain or forecast markets — a month of bad returns teaches only that stocks are risky, not that a pattern is at work ([Jan 2016 Data Update 8](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/02/january-2016-data-update-8-pricing-with.html), 2016-02).

## 4. Myths and mistakes Damodaran calls out

- **"The Fed sets interest rates."** Repeated across nearly every year's rates post: central banks nudge at the margin; the fundamental drivers are expected inflation and real growth, and the intrinsic-rate graph (actual T-bond rate vs. inflation+real growth) tracks the actual rate far better than any Fed-centric story. 2022's rate spike (1.51%→3.88%) happened as inflation surprised, not because the Fed "decided" — the Fed Funds rate hikes followed market rates, not the reverse ([Jan 2016 Update 2](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-2-interest.html), 2016-01; [Data Update 3 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/01/data-update-3-for-2023-inflation-and.html), 2023-01).
- **"Debt lowers your cost of capital because it's cheaper" / "higher ROE from debt is a real benefit."** Both ignore that cost of equity rises with leverage to offset; the only *real* net effect of debt on value is the tax shield versus expected distress costs ([Jan 2017 Update 8](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/02/january-2017-data-update-8-dark-and.html), 2017-02; [Data Update 6 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/02/data-update-6-for-2023-wake-up-call-for.html), 2023-02).
- **The small-cap premium.** Uncovered from 1926–1980 data; essentially vanished on a value-weighted basis after 1980, and turned *negative* on an equal-weighted basis in the 2010s. Damodaran has "never" added a small-cap premium in a valuation and calls the continued practice "inertia and laziness" ([Data Update 2 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/01/data-update-2-for-2020-retrospective-on.html), 2020-01; [Data Update 1 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/01/data-update-1-for-2021-data-look-back.html), 2021-01).
- **The value premium ("low P/B beats high P/B") always works.** Historically real (5.22%/yr average 1927–2019) but the 2010s decade delivered essentially no value premium; Damodaran attributes decay to "lazy value investing" (mechanical low-multiple screens now arbitraged away by index funds/ETFs), not to value investing itself being wrong ([Data Update 2 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/01/data-update-2-for-2020-retrospective-on.html), 2020-01).
- **Big data / crowd wisdom as automatic edges.** Only valuable if the data is *exclusive* (not "everyone has it") and *actionable* (convertible into product/service change) — true for Netflix/Amazon, not for most self-styled "data-driven" startups (MoviePass, Bird cited as counterexamples) ([Jan 2017 Update 1](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-1-promise-and.html), 2017-01; [Data Update 2 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/01/data-update-2-for-2020-retrospective-on.html), 2020-01; [Data Update 1 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/01/data-update-1-for-2022-it-is-moneyball.html), 2022-01).
- **Bitcoin/crypto as a crisis hedge or as an "asset."** 2020 data: Bitcoin and Ethereum correlated 0.43/0.45 with the S&P 500 (behaving like risky stocks, not gold) versus gold/silver at only 0.17/0.24; Damodaran classifies bitcoin as, at best, an unproven currency or a collectible with an uncertain "longevity" case, never a valuable asset in the DCF sense ([Data Update 3 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/01/data-update-3-for-2021-currencies.html), 2021-01).
- **Margin of safety as a distinct risk-adjustment tool, competing with discount-rate risk adjustments.** It's not — it's a buffer applied *after* you already have a value estimate (which itself required a risk-adjusted discount rate), so it can't substitute for a risk measure ([Data Update 5 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/data-update-5-relative-risk-and-hurdle.html), 2020-02).
- **Buyback "fictions" (2023 post, in direct response to the political attacks on Norfolk Southern/Buffett debate):** buybacks alone create or destroy value (they only *redistribute* it — value effects come from how they're funded, not the act itself); buybacks are systematically debt-funded (the 2023 US cross-section shows buyback firms have *lower* debt ratios and lower Debt/EBITDA than non-buyback firms); buybacks are bad for the economy (the cash gets reinvested by someone, just not the paying company — better than a "walking dead" firm reinvesting in a bad business); buybacks are unfair to other stakeholders (a pie-split argument, not a buyback-specific one) ([Data Update 7 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/03/data-update-7-for-2023-dividends.html), 2023-03).
- **Historical ERP as the best forecast of the future.** Backward-looking, assumes mean reversion, and comes with standard errors so large (2.15% for 1928–2022) that ±2 standard errors spans 2.34%–10.94% — not a usable point estimate ([Data Update 2 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/01/data-update-2-for-2023-rocky-year-for.html), 2023-01).
- **Data is objective.** "The most egregious biases are data-backed" — the same tax-rate dataset can support "US companies pay too little tax" (simple average including loss-makers, 14.93% in the 2013 data) or "US companies pay their share" (money-makers only, 28.37%) depending on which slice an advocate selects ([Data Update 1 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/01/data-update-1-for-2020-setting-table.html), 2020-01; [Data Update 2013](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/01/data-update-2013-dark-side-of-numbers.html), 2013-01; [Jan 2017 Update 5](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-5-taxing-year.html), 2017-01; [Data Update 1 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/01/data-update-1-for-2021-data-look-back.html), 2021-01).

## 5. How Damodaran's views evolved (dated)

- **2009-01:** First crisis-era update. ERP jumped from 4.37% (start 2008) to 6.43% (end 2008) — highest since 1978; default spreads on Baa1 bonds more than tripled (1.75%→5.25%); India's ERP rose 7%→11%. Damodaran flags that low reported multiples were an artifact of stale (pre-crisis) accounting numbers scaled against post-crisis prices, not real cheapness ([Data update for January 2009](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/01/data-update-for-january-2009.html), 2009-01; ERP figures restated in [Equity Risk Premiums: An Update](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/10/equity-risk-premiums-update.html), 2009-10).
- **2013-01:** Coins the "dark side of numbers" framework (data to intimidate/mislead/evade responsibility) — a durable meta-lens for reading Damodaran's own and others' data ([Data Update 2013](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/01/data-update-2013-dark-side-of-numbers.html), 2013-01).
- **2016-01:** Adds a payout-adjusted ERP, because trailing-12-month cash returned was 101.54% of S&P 500 earnings — flagged as unsustainable, so Damodaran tapers the cash return to ~84% of earnings over five years; unadjusted ERP 6.12%, adjusted 5.16% ([Jan 2016 Data Update 1](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-1-us-equity.html), 2016-01).
- **2017–2018:** Runs a full valuation-mechanics breakdown of the 2017 US Tax Cuts and Jobs Act *before and after* passage — modeling cash-flow, cost-of-capital, and growth effects separately, estimating a ~9.7% aggregate US equity value increase, and a sector/company matrix of winners (high effective-tax-rate, high capex, low-debt firms) vs. losers (low effective-tax-rate, low-capex, high-debt firms) ([Jan 2017 Update 5](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-5-taxing-year.html), 2017-01; [Data Update 3 for 2018](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/01/january-2018-data-update-3-taxing.html), 2018-01).
- **2019-01:** Publishes the most systematic risk-measurement taxonomy (price/earnings/proxy families) after the Q4-2018 correction reminded markets risk is real; ERP up to 5.96% (Jan 2019), top decile since 1960, exceeded only in 1979, 2009 and 2011 — reads this as the market "more under valued than over valued" ([Data Update 1 for 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/01/january-2019-data-update-1-reminder.html), 2019-01; [Data Update 4 for 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/01/january-2019-data-update-4-many-faces.html), 2019-01).
- **2020 (COVID year):** Data Updates 6 and 7 (both dated March 21, 2020) were written *during* the crisis and caveat that the 2019 profitability numbers are likely to take a major hit from the virus; tracks the ERP daily through the crash (it peaked at 7.75% on March 20, 2020, per the 2021 retrospective). The "Retrospective on a Disruptive Decade" post (2020-01-27) is Damodaran's most explicit statement that the small-cap and value premiums are structurally impaired, not just cyclically dormant ([Data Update 2 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/01/data-update-2-for-2020-retrospective-on.html), 2020-01; [Data Update 6 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/03/data-update-6-for-2020-profitability.html), 2020-03; [Data Update 2 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/01/data-update-2-for-2021-price-of-risk.html), 2021-01).
- **2021:** Full-year post-COVID rebuild — hurdle rates hit generational lows (median US WACC ~5.30%, global ~5.78%); dedicates an entire post to classifying bitcoin/crypto/commodities/collectibles, taking a skeptical but non-dismissive stance ([Data Update 3 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/01/data-update-3-for-2021-currencies.html), 2021-01; [Data Update 4 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/02/data-update-4-for-2021-hurdle-rate.html), 2021-02).
- **2022:** Inflation dominates every post. The Jan 2022 update finds the ERP *fell* over 2021 (4.72%→4.24%) as the index rose, T-bond rates climbed and analysts grew more upbeat about earnings ([Data Update 2 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/01/data-update-2-for-2021-price-of-risk.html), 2021-01; [Data Update 2 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/01/data-update-2-for-2022-us-stocks-kept.html), 2022-01).
- **2023:** The reckoning year. Documents 2022 as the worst nominal *and* real year for 10-year Treasuries in the 1928–2022 database (-17.83% nominal, -22.79% real), a top-7 worst year for stocks, and the *first time* in that dataset that stocks and bonds both had double-digit negative years together. Cost of capital for the median US company nearly doubles (5.77%→9.63%) year over year. Uses the Adani Group/Hindenburg episode as a live case study for country-risk-and-leverage analysis. Responds directly and pointedly to the 2022 Inflation Reduction Act's 1% buyback tax and to political attacks on buybacks (Norfolk Southern derailment blame, the Senate debate) with Damodaran's most detailed buyback fact-check ([Data Update 2 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/01/data-update-2-for-2023-rocky-year-for.html), 2023-01; [Data Update 3 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/01/data-update-3-for-2023-inflation-and.html), 2023-01; [Data Update 6 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/02/data-update-6-for-2023-wake-up-call-for.html), 2023-02; [Data Update 7 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/03/data-update-7-for-2023-dividends.html), 2023-03).

## 7. Cases and examples

- **Adani Enterprises / Adani Group, 2023** — During the Hindenburg short-seller episode, Damodaran computes Adani Enterprises' actual debt (₹413.4bn) at more than double its estimated value-maximizing optimal (₹185.3bn); aggregating across all seven listed Adani entities, the group carries ~3× its optimal debt, illustrating that "control" motives (family/founder unwillingness to dilute via equity) can drive systematically dysfunctional leverage. Lesson: over-levering doesn't require fraud — bad capital-structure discipline is sufficient ([Data Update 6 for 2023](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/02/data-update-6-for-2023-wake-up-call-for.html), 2023-02).
- **Coca-Cola vs. Royal Dutch Shell, country-ERP weighting, 2020** — Coca-Cola's ERP is revenue-weighted across its geographic mix (asset-light, movable production); Royal Dutch's production-weighted; Saudi Aramco's ERP is based entirely on Saudi Arabia's since all extraction is domestic — illustrates that the right weighting variable depends on whether costs/production are geographically "sticky" ([Data Update 4 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/data-update-4-country-risk-and-currency.html), 2020-02).
- **Apple's debt geography, 2017** — Despite a ~21% effective global tax rate (from foreign operations), Apple concentrated its debt issuance in the US, where its marginal tax rate (~40% with state/local) delivers the biggest interest deduction — evidence that the *marginal*, not *effective*, tax rate should drive multinational debt-location and cost-of-debt assumptions ([Jan 2017 Update 5](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/01/january-2017-data-update-5-taxing-year.html), 2017-01).
- **BHP Billiton, "over my dead body," 2015/2017** — CEO Andrew Mackenzie's response when asked about cutting dividends as commodity prices crashed — Damodaran's go-to illustration of dividend stickiness turning into dysfunction, especially in natural-resource sectors with historically high payout commitments ([Jan 2017 Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/02/january-2017-data-update-9-dividends.html), 2017-02).
- **FAANG stocks, 2010s decade** — Facebook/Amazon/Apple/Netflix/Google collectively added ~$3.4 trillion in market cap over 2010–2019, ~22% of the S&P 500's total market-cap increase for the decade; a portfolio with none of the five would have struggled to keep pace with the index — Damodaran's central illustration of "winner-take-most" decade concentration ([Data Update 2 for 2020](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/01/data-update-2-for-2020-retrospective-on.html), 2020-01).
- **Bitcoin/Ethereum, 2020** — Both up massively (Bitcoin +300%+, Ethereum +469%) but behaved like high-beta risky equities during the COVID crash and recovery (correlation with S&P 500 of 0.43/0.45) rather than like crisis hedges (gold: 0.17; silver: 0.24) — used to argue crypto had not yet earned either the "currency" or "collectible" case it needed ([Data Update 3 for 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/01/data-update-3-for-2021-currencies.html), 2021-01).
- **Japan, recurring "walking dead" cash-hoarder case** — Lowest dividend payout ratios globally across years; cash balances reaching ~34% of firm value / ~68% of market cap (2018); read as a symptom of a market where capital stays trapped in declining businesses instead of migrating to better ones, a drag on the broader economy ([Jan 2016 Update 7](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-7-dividends.html), 2016-01; [Data Update 9 for 2018](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/02/january-2018-data-update-9-dividends.html), 2018-02).
- **Tobacco, recurring "best business" despite ESG stigma** — Tops the excess-return rankings repeatedly (2015 and 2021 data) — Damodaran's standing rebuttal to any simple ESG-quality-equals-returns narrative, since the most "sin-stock" sector is consistently among the most profitable relative to its cost of capital ([Jan 2016 Update 5](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/01/january-2016-data-update-5-making-case.html); [Data Update 5 for 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/02/data-update-5-for-2022-bottom-line.html), 2022-02).
- **January 2016 China/oil selloff, real-time pricing case** — Global equities lost $5.54 trillion (≈8.42% of value) in the month, driven by Chinese market declines and falling oil prices, yet the month would not have made the 25 (or even 50) worst-months list in US market history. Mainland Chinese stocks fell >20% in market cap (PE 20.28x→15.73x during the month), while tobacco, utilities, and telecom were the only sectors posting gains and precious metals rallied on flight-to-safety; integrated oil, counterintuitively, was not among the worst-hit industries. Illustrates Damodaran's practice of separating headline drama from historical perspective ([Jan 2016 Data Update 8](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/02/january-2016-data-update-8-pricing-with.html), 2016-02).
- **India's ERP during the 2008 crisis** — Rose from 7% to 11% within the year, which Damodaran said made the Sensex's drop "no surprise" — an early, simple demonstration that country ERP moves are not academic abstractions but track real market drawdowns ([Data update for January 2009](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/01/data-update-for-january-2009.html), 2009-01).
