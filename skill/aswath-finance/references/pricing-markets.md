## 1. Core frameworks and principles

**Value vs. Price — the central distinction.** Value is a function of cash flows, growth and risk, estimated with intrinsic models (DCF); price is set by demand and supply, driven by mood, momentum and liquidity, and estimated by finding comparable assets and a scaling metric ([Venture Capital: pricing not value](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/10/venture-capital-it-is-pricing-not-value.html), 2016-10; [January 2018 Data Update 10](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/02/january-2018-data-update-10-price-is.html), 2018-02; [January 2019 Data Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/02/january-2019-data-update-9-pricing-game.html), 2019-02). The two processes can and do diverge, sometimes for years. Every investment philosophy is a stance on this gap: efficient marketers think it's random noise that closes instantly; value/growth investors believe it closes eventually if you're patient; traders exploit it without caring why it exists ([January 2019 Data Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/02/january-2019-data-update-9-pricing-game.html), 2019-02).

**Investing vs. trading, formalized as a table.** Investing = estimate value, buy/sell if price diverges, wait for convergence (long horizon, tools: ratio analysis, DCF, accounting). Trading = guess price direction, ride momentum, exit before it shifts (short horizon, tools: charts, technical indicators, investor psychology). "The most delusional player" is a trader who thinks he's trading on value, or a value investor who thinks he can reason with markets ([The Bitcoin Boom](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/10/the-bitcoin-boom-asset-currency.html), 2017-10).

**Four asset categories, each priced differently.** (1) *Cash-generating assets* (stocks, bonds, businesses) — can be both valued and priced. (2) *Commodities* (oil, iron ore) — value is theoretically derivable from demand/supply but practically hard, so they get priced against their own history. (3) *Currencies* — no cash flows; can only be priced against other currencies. (4) *Collectibles* (gold, art, baseball cards) — no cash flows, priced on scarcity + demand. Gold is "more currency than commodity" because its price is set by store-of-value demand, not industrial use ([The Bitcoin Boom](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/10/the-bitcoin-boom-asset-currency.html), 2017-10; [Bitcoin Backlash](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/10/bitcoin-backlash-back-to-drawing-board.html), 2017-10; [A Golden Year](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/11/a-golden-year-2025-golds-price-surge.html), 2025-11). Test for whether something is a currency-collectible vs. an asset: does holding it generate cash flow on its own (no), and would its price go to zero if humanity collectively stopped using it (gold: no, because of residual industrial/aesthetic use; a currency with zero adoption: yes) ([Bitcoin Backlash](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/10/bitcoin-backlash-back-to-drawing-board.html), 2017-10).

**Value, Gap and Pricing events.** Corporate/market actions split into three types by mechanism: a *value event* changes cash flows, growth or risk (capacity expansion, M&A). A *gap event* doesn't change value but draws attention to a mispricing so the market corrects it (spin-offs, activist campaigns). A *pricing event* alters mood, momentum or liquidity without touching value or the gap (stock splits, index inclusion, some buybacks). Most real events have a dominant effect plus secondary spillovers ([Illusion, Perception and Reality](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/09/illusion-perception-and-reality-stock.html), 2020-09). An earlier version of this taxonomy, built around round-number "**market triggers**" (a stock crossing $1, $1,000, unicorn status, a trillion-dollar market cap), decomposes the same three channels with sharper mechanism labels: *value effects* (a threshold is contractually tied to management comp, debt covenants, or convertible-conversion terms — e.g., Tesla's 2018 Musk pay package set 12 market-cap tiers and 16 operating (revenue/EBITDA) triggers, each unlocking share grants and diluting existing holders), *pricing effects* (psychological support/resistance-line effects, liquidity effects from stock splits or unicorn-status fundraising, and pure herd effects), and *gap/catalyst effects* (crossing a threshold draws analyst/media attention to a small or under-covered stock, forcing a reassessment that can cut either direction). Damodaran's practical rule for value investors: don't dismiss triggers as noise — a subset have real value consequences, and even the purely pricing-driven ones can function as the catalyst a value investor needs to convert a correct valuation into a realized gain (Damodaran personally watches support/resistance lines on small-cap positions to time entries, without letting them override the underlying value call) ([Trillion Dollar Toppers](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/09/trillion-dollar-toppers-market-triggers.html), 2018-09).

**Corporate breakups: value effect vs. price effect.** A breakup only creates value if the pieces can do something the consolidated firm structurally could not (regulatory constraint, legal contamination, debt covenants, tax code, entrenched culture, dividend clientele mismatch). Otherwise a breakup is purely a *pricing* play: it changes which comparables and which metric the market uses to price the pieces, which matters most for companies with opaque financials, diverse businesses, or a "hot" sub-segment obscured inside a boring parent ([Corporate Breakups](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/10/corporate-breakups-value-and-pricing.html), 2014-10).

**The corporate life cycle and its financial balance sheet.** Firms move from start-up → young growth → high growth → mature growth → decline. Value shifts from mostly "growth assets" (young) to mostly "assets in place" (old); cash flow shifts from cash-burn to cash-accumulation to cash-return. Cash's *purpose* on the balance sheet shifts correspondingly: life-preserver (survival, start-up) → scalar (fund growth) → optionality (mature growth) → youth serum (mature, hoping for reinvention) → liquidation manager (decline) ([A Viral Market Update X](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/06/a-viral-market-update-x-corporate-life.html), 2020-06; [To Bitcoin or not to Bitcoin](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/07/to-bitcoin-or-not-to-bitcoin-corporate.html), 2025-07).

**Flexibility as the unifying quality that wins crises.** Four types: *investment flexibility* (revenue generated per dollar of invested capital — how fast you can scale); *operating flexibility* (fixed vs. variable cost mix, proxied by gross margin); *financing flexibility* (net debt/EBITDA — debt and interest lock in volatility); *cash-return flexibility* (dividends are sticky, buybacks are not, so heavy dividend payers have the least room to adapt). Trade-offs: flexible business models compress the corporate life cycle (faster scale-up, faster decline), give up upside in exchange for downside protection, and sometimes shift real costs onto society (e.g., gig-worker classification) ([A Viral Market Update XI](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/07/a-viral-market-update-xi-flexibility.html), 2020-07).

**Catastrophic risk vs. incremental risk.** Incremental risk changes value by degree; catastrophic risk threatens survival or trajectory. Catastrophic risks vary on three axes: source (nature, human/war, regulatory), locus of damage (single firm vs. sector/economy-wide) and likelihood (usually low-probability but capable of becoming high-probability with time — Iceland's volcanoes, pandemics). Treatment depends on a 2x2+: is it insurable? Is it company-specific or market-wide? (see §2 for the formulas) ([Catastrophic Risk](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/02/catastrophic-risk-investing-and.html), 2024-02).

**The crisis cycle.** Trigger event (economic/political/financial) → immediate market repricing (higher risk premiums, flight to safety) → aftershocks (mostly negative) → real-economy slowdown (near term) → long-term reset of growth/inflation expectations feeding back into markets. Post-mortems are unreliable: investors over-extrapolate "markets always come back," regulators fix symptoms not causes, and researchers cherry-pick data to confirm priors. Each crisis is a sample size of one ([Anatomy of a Market Crisis](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/04/anatomy-of-market-crisis-tariffs-rock.html), 2025-04).

**Disruptive shocks favor simple, decentralized systems over complex, centralized ones — a resilience framework predating (and feeding into) the later catastrophic-risk/crisis-cycle work.** Five lessons drawn from Hurricane Sandy's aftermath, applied to markets: (1) what looks like a rare "accident" the first time may be the new normal the third time — treat recurring macro shocks (banking crisis, Eurozone, etc.) as structural, not aberrant; (2) historical precedent provides little guidance for a genuinely disruptive shock — extrapolating past ERPs/PEs to "normal" is often exactly wrong mid-crisis; (3) an information vacuum during a shock gets filled with rumor, in markets as much as in a blackout; (4) backup systems built for the *last* crisis (new risk-management overlays) can themselves fail against the next, different one; (5) it is better to design resilient systems from the start than to bolt on backups after the fact. Two design principles follow: prefer **simpler over more complex** systems/strategies (Damodaran's own intrinsic-valuation process needs only an annual report and a calculator, and survives a data/technology blackout that would cripple a quant strategy) and **decentralized over centralized** ones (a hub-and-spoke system fails entirely when its hub goes down, as happened to airlines during Sandy) ([Storms and Stocks](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2012/11/storms-and-stocks-dealing-with.html), 2012-11).

**Valuation as a "craft," not an art or a science — and a companion claim that anything can be priced and almost anything can be valued.** Unlike physics, valuation has no immutable laws precise enough to be a science; unlike art, it isn't pure creative license either — it's a craft learned by repeated doing (like cooking or carpentry), which is why Damodaran values a real company every week in class rather than teaching from static cases. The post extends the value-vs-price distinction to a completion claim: nearly any cash-flow-generating asset can be *valued* (Damodaran has valued a professional athlete's earning power, a sports franchise, a "trophy" asset, and pre-revenue young companies), while at minimum, everything — gold, bitcoin, currencies — can be *priced* ([My Valuation Class: Fall 2015 Preview](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/my-valuation-class-fall-2015-model.html), 2015-08).

**The price of risk is the single number that explains most of a crisis.** In equities that's the implied equity risk premium (ERP); in bonds it's the default spread. Damodaran tracks both daily during every crisis (2008, COVID, 2025 tariffs) as the primary read on whether fear or fundamentals are driving the tape ([Another Market Crisis? My Survival Manual](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/another-market-crisis-my-survival.html), 2015-08; [A Viral Market Meltdown](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/a-viral-market-meltdown-fear-or.html), 2020-02; [Anatomy of a Market Crisis](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/04/anatomy-of-market-crisis-tariffs-rock.html), 2025-04).

## 2. Methods and procedures

**Relative valuation done right — a four-step process** ([January 2018 Data Update 10](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/02/january-2018-data-update-10-price-is.html), 2018-02; [January 2019 Data Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/02/january-2019-data-update-9-pricing-game.html), 2019-02):
1. **Find "similar" traded assets** — a subjective judgment; narrow (same market/size) trades focus for fewer comparables, broad (global, cross-sector) trades diversity for a bigger sample.
2. **Pick the pricing metric consistently** — equity value (market cap) must pair with an equity-value denominator (net income, book equity); enterprise value must pair with an operating-value denominator (EBITDA, sales, invested capital). "Price to EBITDA" is "an inconsistent abomination."
3. **Choose the scaling variable and match timing** — trailing vs. forward earnings must be applied consistently across every company in the comparison set; the metric should shift with life-cycle stage (revenue-driver multiples early → equity earnings → EBITDA → book value/liquidation value as a firm ages).
4. **Control for differences** — either subjectively (storytelling) or objectively, by deriving the multiple's fundamental drivers algebraically from a simple DCF (every multiple embeds assumptions about growth, risk, and reinvestment efficiency) and checking whether the company's multiple is justified by those drivers.

**Deriving a multiple's fundamentals from a DCF.** Starting from a stable-growth dividend discount model, PE = f(payout ratio, growth g, cost of equity); Price/Book = f(ROE, growth, cost of equity); EV/EBITDA = f(reinvestment rate, growth, tax rate); EV/Sales = f(after-tax operating margin, growth, cost of capital). This lets you screen for genuine mismatches instead of naive "low multiple = cheap" ([The Price is Right](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/02/january-2018-data-update-10-price-is.html), 2018-02; [Explaining a Paradox](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/03/explaining-paradox-why-good-bad.html), 2017-03; [January 2019 Data Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/02/january-2019-data-update-9-pricing-game.html), 2019-02):

| Multiple | Fundamental driver | "Cheap" mismatch to screen for |
|---|---|---|
| PE | Growth, equity risk, payout | Low PE + high EPS growth |
| PEG | Growth, equity risk, payout | Low PEG + low growth (paradoxically) |
| PBV | ROE, growth, equity risk | Low PBV + high ROE |
| EV/Invested Capital | ROIC, growth, operating risk | Low ratio + high ROIC |
| EV/Sales | After-tax margin, growth, operating risk | Low ratio + high margin |
| EV/EBITDA | Reinvestment rate, growth, tax rate | Low ratio + low reinvestment need |

**Pricing country risk empirically: which multiple actually reflects it, and four ways to use that fact.** Equity multiples (PE, price-to-book) do correlate with country risk in the direction theory predicts — riskier countries trade at lower multiples, holding growth/payout/ROE constant, exactly as the DDM-derived intrinsic-PE/PBV formulas predict — but the relationship is noisy (Venezuela traded at an absurdly *high* PE/PBV in July 2015 despite extreme risk, reflecting collapsing/near-zero earnings and book value more than genuine mispricing). Enterprise-value multiples (EV/EBITDA) show much *weaker* correlation with country risk, because they strip out the financial-leverage differences equity multiples conflate with risk — the optimistic reading is that EV/EBITDA is the more defensible multiple for cross-country comparison; the pessimistic reading is that using EV/EBITDA cross-country will systematically overvalue emerging-market companies, since that multiple is less likely to have country risk already priced into it (India and China traded at the *highest* EV/EBITDA multiples globally in July 2015, despite non-trivial country risk). Four ways to build a pricing comparison that accounts for this: (1) compare stocks within one risky country (assumes uniform country-risk exposure across all local firms — often false); (2) compare stocks within one sector within one country (assumes country and sector risk are both priced in, but shrinks your sample, especially in small markets); (3) compare stocks within a sector globally, ignoring country risk entirely; (4) compare within a sector globally while explicitly controlling for the developed-vs-emerging differential. In resolution, Damodaran trusts personal valuation skills more than pricing instincts ("I am more investor than trader") and defaults to intrinsic value, but recommends running both — value *and* price — and buying only when both signal the stock is a bargain ([Pricing Country Risk – Pictures of Global Risk Part III](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/07/pricing-country-risk-pictures-of-global.html), 2015-07).

**How pricing multiples behave mechanically across the three phases of a crisis — and why "it looks cheap/expensive on a trailing multiple" is unreliable in each one** ([A Viral Market Update VII: Mayhem with Multiples](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/04/a-viral-market-update-vii-mayhem-with.html), 2020-04): **Phase 1** — price drops immediately on the crisis shock, but the scaling variable (trailing earnings/revenue/book value) hasn't caught up yet (accounting lags reality, and few companies have withdrawn or updated guidance), so trailing multiples mechanically shrink and stocks look artificially "cheap" even with no real repricing of fundamentals. **Phase 2** — as guidance resumes, analysts and companies shift to forward estimates; because more firms are loss-making mid-crisis, revenue multiples proliferate as substitutes for now-meaningless forward P/E. **Phase 3** — trailing operating numbers finally catch up and fully reflect crisis damage, at which point trailing multiples can look artificially *expensive* relative to historical norms (or simply not meaningful, for firms still near breakeven), even though nothing is actually overpriced. The lesson generalizes Damodaran's "Trojan Horse DCF" critique: there is no mechanical shortcut around forecasting judgment during a crisis — pricing requires exactly the same forward assumptions intrinsic valuation does, just implicitly rather than explicitly.

**Using absolute vs. relative pricing rules.** Never apply a fixed cutoff ("PE under 6 is cheap," "EV/EBITDA under 6 means LBO candidate") without checking it against the *current* distribution — cutoffs must be rescaled to prevailing market conditions (interest rates, risk premiums) or they systematically flag the riskiest countries/sectors as "bargains" ([January 2019 Data Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/02/january-2019-data-update-9-pricing-game.html), 2019-02).

**Implied Equity Risk Premium (ERP), computed daily in crises.** Take trailing-12-month cash flows to equity investors (dividends + buybacks), forecast expected growth, solve for the internal rate of return (IRR) that makes the PV of those cash flows equal the current index level, then subtract the risk-free (T-bond) rate. During crises, Damodaran recomputes this every trading day, sometimes with a "normalized" cash-flow variant if trailing payouts look unsustainable ([Another Market Crisis?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/another-market-crisis-my-survival.html), 2015-08; [A Viral Market Meltdown](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/a-viral-market-meltdown-fear-or.html), 2020-02; [Anatomy of a Market Crisis](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/04/anatomy-of-market-crisis-tariffs-rock.html), 2025-04).

**Valuing the market/index intrinsically.** Same DCF logic applied to the S&P 500 as a whole: base-year cash flows (dividends+buybacks), near-term earnings shock and long-term earnings recovery assumption, cash-payout-ratio assumption, and an ERP (either backed out or assumed) — run as a full valuation with a Monte Carlo simulation around the uncertain inputs rather than a single point estimate ([A Viral Market Meltdown](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/02/a-viral-market-meltdown-fear-or.html), 2020-02; [A Viral Market Update IX: DIY S&P 500](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/06/a-viral-market-update-ix-do-it-yourself.html), 2020-06; [A Fed Chair Opines on Stocks](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/10/a-fairly-highly-valued-market-fed-chair.html), 2025-10). Damodaran's earliest worked version of this same index-DCF (March 2013) breaks the S&P 500's value into four variables tracked separately in every later update — cash returned to equity investors (dividends+buybacks), expected growth (checked against an intrinsic sustainable-growth cross-check, `Equity reinvestment rate × Return on equity`, so analyst growth forecasts can't run away from fundamentals), the riskfree rate, and the ERP — then swaps the then-current implied ERP for its trailing-decade average to show how much of the "market looks expensive" story was really a risk-premium story rather than a cash-flow one ([A Sweet Spot for US Equities](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/03/a-sweet-spot-for-us-equities.html), 2013-03).

**Catastrophic-risk valuation formulas** ([Catastrophic Risk](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/02/catastrophic-risk-investing-and.html), 2024-02):
- Insurable risk → simply expense the insurance premium (even if not actually purchased, estimate its cost) and leave the discount rate untouched.
- Uninsurable, company survives → `Expected Value = Value w/o Catastrophe × (1 − P) + Value w/ Catastrophe × P`, run as two separate going-concern scenarios (don't try to blend growth/risk assumptions into one model).
- Uninsurable, company fails → same formula but the catastrophe branch uses *failure/liquidation value*, not a distressed going-concern value; never try to capture failure risk by simply raising the discount rate — "discount rates are blunt instruments."
- Market-wide/sector-wide catastrophic risk → adjust the discount rate too (beta or ERP), since this portion is non-diversifiable; industry-average betas and the daily implied ERP are Damodaran's preferred dynamic proxies (bank beta jumped from 0.71 in 2007 to 0.85 by 2009 post-crisis regulation).

**Testing whether market timing works — a three-step backtest recipe** ([A Fed Chair Opines on Stocks](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/10/a-fairly-highly-valued-market-fed-chair.html), 2025-10): (1) choose a pricing metric (price change, PE/CAPE, ERP/intrinsic value); (2) set an action threshold (e.g., act if CAPE is >25% above/below its trailing 50-year median); (3) define the response (shift equity allocation from a 60/40 base mix to, say, 80/20 or 40/60). Backtest against a static-allocation benchmark using real historical data (Damodaran used Shiller's 1871–2025 dataset), including transaction costs and taxes conceptually even when excluded from the base case.

**A market-timer statistical significance test.** `t = (proportion of calls correct) / (0.5 / √n)`. With small samples (most market gurus make 15–25 calls in a lifetime), it's very hard to reject the null hypothesis that a "successful" timer was just lucky (15/25 correct → t = 1.00, not significant) ([Macro and Market Timers](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/06/macro-and-market-timers.html), 2009-06).

**Gold/collectible pricing regressions** — three drivers, tested each on annual data 1963–2024/25 ([The Golden Rule](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/04/the-golden-rule-thoughts-on-gold-as.html), 2013-04; [A Golden Year](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/11/a-golden-year-2025-golds-price-surge.html), 2025-11): inflation, the implied ERP (fear of crisis, equity side), and real interest rates (T-bond rate minus realized inflation). None alone explains much (R² 5%–22%); gold is a hedge against *extreme/unexpected* inflation and *catastrophic* risk events, not garden-variety volatility. A gold/CPI ratio regressed on ERP gives a relative-value benchmark you can compare to the historical median.

**"Bludgeon, not scalpel": how to use large-sample industry-average pricing data responsibly.** When building comparables/multiples datasets across tens of thousands of companies (as opposed to valuing one company), precision has to give way to workable approximation — e.g., proxying cost of debt from stock-price volatility for the whole sample rather than pulling an actual/synthetic bond rating for each firm. Four practical caveats when using such aggregated data: (1) the underlying classifications and risk proxies embed the compiler's subjective judgment, not objective fact; (2) go to primary company filings, not the aggregated dataset, when valuing one specific company — the dataset is for sector-level context only; (3) large samples will contain data-entry errors and absurd ratios (a 20,000% ROE, a 0.1 PE) that reflect bad raw inputs, not real outliers to explain away; (4) the law of large numbers is the offsetting benefit — with hundreds of firms per sector, individual outliers barely move the aggregate average, so sector-level multiples/margins remain usable even though single-company line items may not be ([Numbers Time! Data update for 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/01/numbers-time-data-update-for-2014.html), 2014-01).

## 3. Rules of thumb and heuristics

- Multiples/pricing rules only make sense scaled to the *current* distribution, never as fixed absolute cutoffs — "Absolute rules don't belong in a relative world" ([January 2019 Data Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/02/january-2019-data-update-9-pricing-game.html), 2019-02).
- Book value is "the most overrated metric in investing" — it's not a reliable proxy for liquidation value in most sectors, and low P/B stocks cluster in capital-intensive, low-ROE, declining industries, not bargains ([January 2019 Data Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/02/january-2019-data-update-9-pricing-game.html), 2019-02).
- "Most stocks that look cheap deserve to be cheap" — always check the fundamental driver before concluding a low multiple is a mispricing ([January 2019 Data Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/02/january-2019-data-update-9-pricing-game.html), 2019-02).
- In pricing, "it is not about what should be priced in, but what is priced in" — if the market is pricing a sector on user counts or subscribers instead of earnings, use that metric even if you find it philosophically unsatisfying, because that is the game being played ([January 2019 Data Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/02/january-2019-data-update-9-pricing-game.html), 2019-02).
- Six "bubbler" archetypes to discount when reading market commentary: Doomsday, Knee-Jerk, Armchair Psychiatrist, Conspiratorial, Righteous, and Rational bubblers — even the "rational" ones fall in love with a single metric and treat mean reversion as immutable ([Bubble, Bubble, Toil and Trouble](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/bubble-bubble-toil-and-trouble-costs.html), 2014-06).
- A bubble is a market disconnect from fundamentals — prices rising because cash flows, growth or risk actually improved is *not* a bubble, no matter how steep the rise ([Bubble, Bubble, Toil and Trouble](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/bubble-bubble-toil-and-trouble-costs.html), 2014-06).
- Acting on a suspected bubble only pays off with high conviction, a large expected correction, and a short expected wait; with 20% conviction, it "almost never pays to act" ([Bubble, Bubble, Toil and Trouble](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/bubble-bubble-toil-and-trouble-costs.html), 2014-06).
- The "sleep test": if you lie awake worrying about your portfolio, your strategy/allocation doesn't fit your personal make-up, regardless of its theoretical soundness ([A Viral Market Meltdown III](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/03/a-viral-market-meltdown-iii-pricing-or.html), 2020-03; [Anatomy of a Market Crisis](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/04/anatomy-of-market-crisis-tariffs-rock.html), 2025-04).
- Great company ≠ great investment, and vice versa: separately assess (a) business quality (excess returns over cost of capital), (b) management quality (value of control = optimal value − status quo value), and (c) price vs. value; only the last determines whether to buy ([Explaining a Paradox](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/03/explaining-paradox-why-good-bad.html), 2017-03).
- If you can't value an asset (currency, collectible, crypto), you can only trade it, never "invest" in it in the value sense — don't confuse the two skill sets ([The Bitcoin Boom](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/10/the-bitcoin-boom-asset-currency.html), 2017-10).
- Macro/market-timing bets need a genuine edge (time horizon, trading capability, information, or a behavioral quirk you're immune to) — otherwise you're just adding noise ([Macro Bets: A general framework](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/11/macro-bets-general-framework.html), 2009-11).
- Keep a macro bet simple and direct (buy the futures/options on the thing itself); combining a macro view with a stock pick (buying a gold miner because you like gold prices) mixes in unrelated company-specific risk ([Macro Bets](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/11/macro-bets-general-framework.html), 2009-11).
- When valuing commodity/cyclical companies, stay commodity-price-neutral (use forward market prices, not your own commodity view) so the analyst's macro call and stock-specific call can be judged separately ([Commodity companies](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/08/commodity-companies-and-commodity.html), 2009-08).
- During a crisis: break the portfolio-checking feedback loop, turn off financial news, revisit your investment-philosophy "faith," and act via pre-set limit orders rather than in-the-moment panic trades ([Another Market Crisis?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/another-market-crisis-my-survival.html), 2015-08; [An October Surprise](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/10/an-october-surprise-making-sense-of.html), 2018-10; [Anatomy of a Market Crisis](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/04/anatomy-of-market-crisis-tariffs-rock.html), 2025-04).
- **Markets cannot be regulated into perfect fairness, and an informationally-disadvantaged trader has only four real options**, illustrated with a Pokemon-card-trading parable about a precociously well-read six-year-old ("HFT") who out-traded the neighborhood kids: (1) invest in matching the advantaged party's edge (expensive, often not worth it for small players); (2) reorient toward trading against parties *less* informed than you, rather than more (morally uncomfortable, and Damodaran explicitly rejects it); (3) become an intermediary supplying liquidity for a fee instead of trading on information; (4) exit the game for that asset. Applied to real high-frequency trading, the parallel four choices are: pay for the technology and compete; shift your trading window away from the announcement moment (where speed advantages are largest) toward the post-announcement drift (where they aren't); become a liquidity-supplying market maker; or stop trading and become an investor instead, since HFT "takes the worst aspects of trading... and magnifies them," meaning value investors are largely unaffected by (and may even benefit from) an HFT-heavy market. The post also notes the advantage is self-eroding: the six-year-old's edge vanished within a year as more neighborhood kids learned to read, and he was later found unloading his card collection at a garage sale for pennies — a reminder that informational edges in a pricing (not value) game decay as competitors catch up, and neglecting fundamentals eventually catches up with even a successful trader ([The impossible quest for fair markets](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/03/the-impossible-quest-for-fair-markets.html), 2014-03).
- A gold-as-insurance allocation only matters if it's big enough — 5% of a portfolio is not enough to hedge catastrophic risk; historically you'd need something closer to 15–20% ([A Golden Year](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/11/a-golden-year-2025-golds-price-surge.html), 2025-11).

## 4. Myths and mistakes Damodaran calls out

- **"The efficient market hypothesis caused the 2008 crisis" (Jeremy Grantham, 2009).** Damodaran dismantles this point by point: bubbles predate EMH by centuries (tulip mania, South Sea Bubble); the financial sector's entire securitization/M&A business model presumes markets are inefficient, not efficient; compensation incentives and complex instruments only make sense if people believed they *could* beat mispriced markets ([Culprit found for market crisis](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/06/this-collapse-is-due-to-efficient.html), 2009-06).
- **Trojan Horse DCFs.** Analysts dress up a pricing exercise as a "DCF" by estimating near-term cash flows carefully, then smuggling a terminal-value *multiple* (a pure pricing input) into the biggest single cash flow in the model, letting it masquerade as intrinsic value ([January 2018 Data Update 10](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/02/january-2018-data-update-10-price-is.html), 2018-02).
- **CAPE/Shiller PE overconfidence.** Tested against trailing PE, normalized PE and CAPE from 1969–2016: the four measures move together most of the time (correlation close to one between normalized PE and CAPE, so CAPE isn't uniquely informative); CAPE's predictive power for one-year returns is low, and trailing PE predicts one-year returns better; a naive CAPE-based market-timing rule back-tested 1927–2016 *destroys* portfolio value as you trust it more; and CAPE ignores interest-rate context entirely — comparing EP ratio (1/PE) to the T-bond rate reverses the "overvalued" conclusion in low-rate eras ([Superman and Stocks](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/08/superman-and-stocks-it-not-cape-cape-it.html), 2016-08). The 2025 CAPE backtest across 1871–2025 data confirms the null result generalizes: no threshold/action combination tested added to annual returns over 1975–2024 ([A Fed Chair Opines on Stocks](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/10/a-fairly-highly-valued-market-fed-chair.html), 2025-10).
- **"Markets have gone crazy, disconnected from the economy."** Quarterly stock returns and real GDP growth have almost no *contemporaneous* correlation historically (markets are forward-looking predictive mechanisms, not real-time economic gauges); the correlation only strengthens looking 3–4 quarters *ahead*, and even then plateaus around 0.26 ([A Viral Market Update X](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/06/a-viral-market-update-x-corporate-life.html), 2020-06; [A Viral Market Update XIV](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/11/a-viral-market-update-xiv-wrap-on-covid.html), 2020-11).
- **The Fed as market puppet-master.** In both the 2008 and COVID crises, most of the drop in T-bond yields happened *before* the Fed's headline QE announcements; the Fed's real effect was as a lending-market backstop for corporate credit spreads, not a rate-setter ([A Viral Market Update IX](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/06/a-viral-market-update-ix-do-it-yourself.html), 2020-06; [Anatomy of a Market Crisis](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/04/anatomy-of-market-crisis-tariffs-rock.html), 2025-04). Low rates cut both ways for valuation: they lower the discount rate but also signal lower long-run growth (Damodaran ties the terminal growth rate to the risk-free rate).
- **The diversification sales pitch was oversold.** Cross-market equity correlations rose sharply after the 1990s pitch was made, and securitizing real estate tied it much more closely to equity markets — the diversification benefit that was sold to a generation of investors is real but far smaller than advertised ([Diversification: Why is it not working?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2008/10/diversification-why-is-it-not-working.html), 2008-10).
- **Stock splits and index inclusion as fundamental catalysts.** Splits are "a purely cosmetic action" with zero value effect (slicing a pizza into more pieces); any price pop is a pricing effect (momentum-feeding, or occasionally a liquidity effect for genuinely under-followed names) — not relevant to Apple/Tesla-scale, already-hyper-followed stocks. Index inclusion studies show gains are often explained by the *pre-existing momentum* that got a stock added, not the addition itself, and the effect has shrunk over time even as passive investing has grown ([Illusion, Perception and Reality](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/09/illusion-perception-and-reality-stock.html), 2020-09).
- **"Trophy asset" labeling as lazy analysis.** Calling something a trophy asset is often a way to avoid explaining a price with real numbers; before assuming a buyer overpaid, check for (1) under-utilization upside, (2) genuine synergies, (3) real optionality — the Financial Times sale largely justified its price on fundamentals; the Economist stake sale to the Agnellis looked more like an actual premium-for-glamor purchase ([Storied Asset Sales](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/valuing-and-pricing-trophy-assets.html), 2015-08).
- **Relative valuation as currently practiced is often "a sign of laziness."** Analysts average multiples and throw away the very data (differences across companies) that would let them draw real lessons about mispricing ([The dangers of relative valuation](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/09/dangers-of-relative-valuation.html), 2009-09).
- **Mark Cuban's "private-market bubble is worse" thesis (2015)** fails on multiple counts: tech's share of market cap (~20%) is nowhere near the 1999 peak (~30%) and is backed by real cash flow share; illiquidity (private markets) is a continuum, not a public/private binary, and low-float young public techs are themselves illiquid; and lab evidence (Vernon Smith) shows *adding* liquidity to a market makes bubbles *bigger*, not smaller — so if anything, illiquid private markets restrain a bubble rather than inflate it ([Illiquidity and Bubbles in Private Share Markets](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/03/illiquidity-and-bubbles-in-private.html), 2015-03).
- **Bitcoin as "millennial gold" during crises.** Empirically false through COVID and the 2025 tariff shock — bitcoin has repeatedly moved *with* risky equities (falling more in the selloff, rising more in the recovery), not as an uncorrelated crisis hedge, though this could still change as it matures ([A Viral Market Meltdown Part II](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/03/a-viral-market-meltdown-part-ii-clues.html), 2020-03; [To Bitcoin or not to Bitcoin](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/07/to-bitcoin-or-not-to-bitcoin-corporate.html), 2025-07).
- **Corporate bitcoin treasuries as a cash substitute.** A "terrible idea" for most companies: bitcoin's volatility is the opposite of what a cash shock-absorber needs; it muddies the operating narrative; it trusts managers (generally poor traders) to time crypto; and if you actually believe in bitcoin, the logical move is to have the company return the cash to you and buy bitcoin yourself, not delegate the trade ([To Bitcoin or not to Bitcoin](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/07/to-bitcoin-or-not-to-bitcoin-corporate.html), 2025-07).

## 5. How Damodaran's views evolved

- **2008 crisis, in real time (Sept–Dec 2008).** Initial posts frame the bailout as fundamentally a liquidity crisis, not a valuation one, and argue for pricing TARP purchases at DCF-derived "fair value," not face value, with warrants/equity kicker for taxpayers (Buffett-Goldman deal as the template) ([The Bailout](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2008/09/bailout.html), 2008-09). By December Damodaran lists lessons "learned, unlearned, and reinforced," including admitting there is no truly riskless asset and that ERPs can move dramatically even in mature markets ([The Crisis of 2008](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2008/12/crisis-of-2008-lessons-learned.html), 2008-12).
- **One year later (Sept 2009).** Retrospective: more comfortable with behavioral finance having "a place at the table," more skeptical of blind mean-reversion on historical data, and a harder-won appreciation that "risk cannot be ever fully measured or controlled" ([Crisis Lessons: Presentation](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/10/crisis-lessons-presentation.html), 2009-10; [One year later](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/09/one-year-later-lessons-from-crisis.html), 2009-09).
- **Relative valuation methodology matures 2009→2018→2019.** Early complaint (2009) that most relative valuation is "lazy" and discards data ([Dangers of relative valuation](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/09/dangers-of-relative-valuation.html), 2009-09) crystallizes by 2018–2019 into the fully worked-out 4-step process (similar assets → consistent metric → matched scaling variable → controlling for fundamental differences), applied globally with actual global multiple datasets ([January 2018 Data Update 10](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/02/january-2018-data-update-10-price-is.html), 2018-02; [January 2019 Data Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/02/january-2019-data-update-9-pricing-game.html), 2019-02).
- **Bitcoin: from open-minded Q&A (2014) → firmer framework (2017) → settled skepticism with narrow carve-outs (2025).** 2014: genuinely undecided, frames it as "bubble or breakthrough? Both!" and evaluates it on trust/acceptance/security as a candidate currency ([Bitcoin Q&A](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/03/bitcoin-q-bubble-or-breakthrough-both.html), 2014-03). 2017: settles on "bitcoin is a currency, not an asset — you can price it, not value it," and lays out three scenarios (global digital currency / gold-for-millennials / 21st-century tulip bulb) ([The Crypto Currency Debate](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/08/the-crypto-currency-debate-future-of.html), 2017-08; [The Bitcoin Boom](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/10/the-bitcoin-boom-asset-currency.html), 2017-10; [Bitcoin Backlash](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/10/bitcoin-backlash-back-to-drawing-board.html), 2017-10). 2025: with bitcoin above $110,000, Damodaran is unchanged on the classification but has fully fleshed out when corporate bitcoin-holding is defensible (bitcoin-savant CEO, bitcoin-native business, failed-currency-country escape, meme-stock strategy) ([To Bitcoin or not to Bitcoin](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/07/to-bitcoin-or-not-to-bitcoin-corporate.html), 2025-07).
- **Gold: skeptic throughout, but the "why it's overpriced" analysis sharpens.** 2008: "always been deeply skeptical" of non-cash-flow assets but concedes gold's crisis-insurance role ([Gold, fine art and collectibles](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2008/10/gold-fine-art-and-collectibles.html), 2008-10). 2013: builds the three-factor regression framework (inflation, ERP, real rates) after the historic one-day 9% gold price drop ([The Golden Rule](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/04/the-golden-rule-thoughts-on-gold-as.html), 2013-04). 2025: reruns the same regressions after gold's rise to $4,000+/oz and concludes gold looks overpriced on every historical metric, but treats a decade-long "overpricing" as possible evidence of a genuine demand-side structural shift (ETF access, mistrust of central banks, dollar erosion) rather than dismissing it ([A Golden Year](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/11/a-golden-year-2025-golds-price-surge.html), 2025-11).
- **Market-crisis playbook: consistent core, expanding toolkit.** The 2015 "survival manual" (break the feedback loop, turn off noise, revisit faith, act via limit orders) ([Another Market Crisis?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/another-market-crisis-my-survival.html), 2015-08) is essentially unchanged in the 2025 tariff-crisis post, but by 2025 it's explicitly generalized into the "crisis cycle" model and paired with formal backtesting of contrarian/timing strategies rather than pure narrative ([Anatomy of a Market Crisis](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/04/anatomy-of-market-crisis-tariffs-rock.html), 2025-04).
- **COVID series (14 posts, Feb–Nov 2020) traces a live view revision:** starts fear-vs-fundamentals (Feb 26) → adds market-damage forensics by sector/region/leverage (Mar) → introduces the flexibility and corporate-life-cycle lenses (Jun–Jul) → documents FANGAM's outsized share of the recovery (Aug) → final wrap admits the market's V-shaped call looks better than the bears', while flagging that markets are "noisy predictors... wrong as often as right" ([A Viral Market Update XIV](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/11/a-viral-market-update-xiv-wrap-on-covid.html), 2020-11).

## 7. Cases and examples

- **Hurricane Sandy (Oct–Nov 2012).** Personal/real-world disruption used to derive Damodaran's resilience framework: repeated multi-day power losses reframed as a possible new normal rather than a fluke, an information vacuum filled by neighborhood rumor, and hub-based airline networks (Newark for United, LaGuardia for Delta) failing entirely when their hub went down — mapped directly onto why investment strategies and risk systems built for "normal" markets should expect, not dismiss, recurring macro shocks ([Storms and Stocks](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2012/11/storms-and-stocks-dealing-with.html), 2012-11).
- **Tesla's 2018 Musk pay package, as a "market trigger" value effect.** Tied share/option grants to 12 market-capitalization tiers and 16 operating metrics (8 revenue, 8 EBITDA) — a rare, explicit real-world case where crossing a round-number valuation threshold has a *genuine*, contractually-defined value effect (dilution) rather than a purely psychological one ([Trillion Dollar Toppers](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2018/09/trillion-dollar-toppers-market-triggers.html), 2018-09).
- **Apple and Amazon crossing $1 trillion market cap (Aug–Sept 2018).** Neither company had options/convertibles keyed to the threshold, so no value effect; pricing effect was directionally ambiguous (some long-term holders selling into the "milestone," some sideline capitulation buyers) and gap/catalyst effect was minimal, since both were already maximally covered — used to show that a market trigger's effect is conditional on a company's specific contractual and coverage circumstances, not a generic function of the round number itself (same source).
- **COVID crisis cross-sectional multiple/leverage findings, April 2020 update.** Contrary to a naive "value stocks should hold up better" expectation, the market punished *low*-PE, high-dividend-yield, low-momentum stocks more than high-PE, non-dividend, high-momentum ones during the Feb–April 2020 selloff — an early data point for the COVID-era "value investing has lost its way" reassessment developed later in 2020. The one variable that did behave as "financial moralists" would predict: firms with higher net debt/EBITDA suffered materially larger losses, confirming financing flexibility (or its absence) as a real crisis discriminator ([A Viral Market Update VII](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/04/a-viral-market-update-vii-mayhem-with.html), 2020-04).
- **Bear Stearns → Lehman → AIG (2008).** Chronicles the mechanical chain: housing-price optimism mispriced every layer of MBS most at the riskiest tranches → mark-to-market write-downs shrank book equity → regulatory capital screens flagged the firms → the equity-raise window (available late 2007) closed by mid-2008 → Bear was rescued, Lehman was not, AIG had to be. Lesson: the failure that should have been questioned was *saving* Bear (it removed the "too risky to save" discipline that might have prevented Lehman) ([I. What happened?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2008/09/i-what-happened.html), 2008-09; [One year later](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2009/09/one-year-later-lessons-from-crisis.html), 2009-09).
- **Buffett–Goldman Sachs/GE deals (Oct 2008).** Buffett got a discounted structure (preferred + warrants) from two of the most "admired" firms in the market; the deals weren't really about the capital, they were about renting Buffett's remaining credibility to signal trust to the market — a pure "gap"/pricing play by the issuers ([The Buffett Gambit](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2008/10/buffett-gambit-buying-selling.html), 2008-10).
- **Harman International LBO (Goldman/KKR, pre-crisis).** Cited as a textbook bad LBO target: no meaningful spare debt capacity, well-managed already (no restructuring upside), no real agency-cost/going-private rationale — used to show that even elite sponsors can be swept up in herd mentality when debt is cheap ([Blackstone's Woes](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2008/11/blackstones-woes-some-thoughts-on.html), 2008-11).
- **GoPro (Oct 2014).** Intrinsic valuation (market-share build-up: total addressable market → GoPro's realistic share → margin fade) put fair value around $32–33/share against a market price of $70; but pricing GoPro as a *social-media* company (not a camera or electronics company) against Facebook/Twitter/LinkedIn multiples came far closer to the market price — a clean live illustration of the value/price gap and of comparable-set selection driving the conclusion ([Go Pro](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/10/go-pro-camera-or-smartphone-social.html), 2014-10).
- **Twitter IPO (2013) referenced repeatedly** as the case for "imputed revenue" pricing: backing out what growth rate or user-value multiple the market is implicitly paying, then judging plausibility against total addressable market size, rather than using a conventional DCF alone. The method's origin: a breakeven-revenue DCF run on Google's October 2013 enterprise value ($240.6bn) — holding operating margin, cost of capital and sales-to-capital ratio fixed — backed out the 2023 revenue Google would need to justify its price, then the same template applied across the social-media/online-advertising group (including Twitter) summed to $319.2bn of imputed 2023 online-ad revenue, above Damodaran's own $303bn top-down market forecast — evidence the sector was collectively overvalued even if each name looked defensible standing alone ([When the pieces add up to too much: Micro Dreams and Macro Delusions](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/10/when-pieces-dont-add-up-micro-dreams.html), 2013-10; [January 2019 Data Update 9](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/02/january-2019-data-update-9-pricing-game.html), 2019-02).
- **HP and eBay breakups (2014).** Used to separate genuine value creation (does the breakup solve a real constraint — regulatory, legal, covenant, tax, culture?) from pure re-rating (does the split just let the market apply a richer multiple to a previously-obscured segment?) ([Corporate Breakups](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/10/corporate-breakups-value-and-pricing.html), 2014-10).
- **Financial Times & Economist sales (2015).** Nikkei's FT price (£844M vs. an estimated status-quo value of £410M) was a much larger premium than Exor's price for the Economist Group (£952M vs. ~£800M estimated), but the FT premium had more possible fundamental justifications (under-utilization, synergy with Asia growth, digital option value); for the Economist, the Agnellis' non-controlling stake and Exor's status as a holding company rule out the control and synergy rationales, leaving only optionality ([Storied Asset Sales](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/valuing-and-pricing-trophy-assets.html), 2015-08).
- **Steve Ballmer / LA Clippers ($2 billion, 2014), referenced as the template case** for "expensive toy" pricing when a buyer's estimated cash-flow-based value ($1.2B) is dwarfed by the price paid — used repeatedly as the sports-franchise/trophy-asset yardstick ([Storied Asset Sales](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/valuing-and-pricing-trophy-assets.html), 2015-08).
- **Brexit vote (June 2016).** Betting markets (Betfair) never priced Leave above 40% even as polls tightened, evidence that big London-based bettors' confirmation bias skewed odds — "markets are weighted, biased counting machines." Post-vote, UK/EU equities and GBP cratered while global spillover was muted relative to 2008, illustrating a *regional* rather than *systemic* crisis pattern ([The Brexit Effect](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/06/the-brexit-effect-looking-for-signals.html), 2016-06).
- **MicroStrategy (2014–2025).** Its stock outperformance is now almost entirely explained by its bitcoin holdings, not its stagnant software business — enterprise value rose from ~$1B (2014) to >$100B (2025) as the stock-price correlation with bitcoin flipped from slightly negative (2014–18) to tightly tracking (recent years); used as the sole clean "bitcoin savant CEO" carve-out for corporate bitcoin treasuries, contrasted with Microsoft's shareholders correctly rejecting a similar proposal ([To Bitcoin or not to Bitcoin](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/07/to-bitcoin-or-not-to-bitcoin-corporate.html), 2025-07).
- **COVID's "resilient risk capital" thesis (2020).** Unlike 2008, VC funding and high-yield bond issuance never really shut down in 2020 — explaining why young/high-growth/high-debt "risk-on" companies *gained* value relative to old/low-growth/low-debt "risk-off" companies during the crisis, the opposite of the conventional flight-to-safety script ([A Viral Market Update XII](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/07/a-viral-market-update-xii-resilience-of.html), 2020-07; [A Viral Market Update XIV](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2020/11/a-viral-market-update-xiv-wrap-on-covid.html), 2020-11).
- **2025 tariff shock as a live case study of the "crisis cycle" framework.** Trigger (April 2 "Liberation Day" tariffs, sized off bilateral trade deficits, not reciprocal tariff rates) → 2-day ~9–10% equity drawdown, muted flight-to-quality (Treasuries barely moved, gold/bitcoin held but didn't spike) → partial reversal through April as tariff threats were walked back → sovereign CDS spreads on the US rose and stayed elevated even after the shock faded, a genuinely new signal (declining trust in US institutions) not seen in 2008 or COVID ([Anatomy of a Market Crisis](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/04/anatomy-of-market-crisis-tariffs-rock.html), 2025-04; [The Greed & Fear Tango](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/05/the-greed-fear-tango-markets-in-april.html), 2025-05).
- **Risk indicators contradicting each other, mid-2017.** The VIX and its European analog sat near historic lows in the months after Brexit and the Trump election even as an independently-constructed Economic Policy Uncertainty Index (built from news-story counts, CBO temporary-tax-provision lists, and forecaster disagreement) spiked past its 2008-crisis levels — direct evidence that a market-based fear gauge and a policy-based uncertainty gauge can point in opposite directions at the same time. Damodaran offers four non-exclusive explanations: markets have grown inured to serial predicted crises that keep not detonating; economic-policy talk consistently overstates governments' actual follow-through; post-2008 stock-return correlation (systematic risk share) had started falling back toward firm-specific risk as investors priced tax/infrastructure policy differently across companies; and — the most novel diagnosis — investors' *political* priors were visibly bleeding into their *market* views (Trump supporters skewing bullish, opponents skewing bearish), which is treated as a distinct, non-fundamental source of pricing noise. Damodaran's own tie-breaker stayed the implied ERP (5.29% on June 1, 2017, an expected return of 7.50% against a 2.21% T-bond rate), used to avoid being talked "onto the cliff or off it" by either narrative ([A Tale of Two Markets: Politics and Investing!](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/06/a-tale-of-two-markets-politics-and.html), 2017-06).
- **Mid-2023 "soft landing" surprise and the return of concentration.** Coming into 2023 off the worst bond-market year in a century and an S&P 500 down ~20% (Nasdaq down about a third) in 2022, consensus expected persistent inflation and recession; by July 1, inflation had fallen and the economy had avoided recession without Fed help (long-term Treasury rates fell even as the Fed hiked), pulling the implied ERP from a 5.94% peak (Jan 1, 2023) down to 5.00% (still roughly at the post-2008 average) and the expected return on stocks from 9.82% to 8.81%. The rally was narrow: seven mega-cap tech names (Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon, Tesla, Meta, Alphabet) added $4.14 trillion, ~80% of the entire increase in market cap across all 6,669 US-listed equities, and money-making companies in the largest-market-cap decile alone accounted for 97.2% of the $5.45 trillion total gain — a "big tech, profitable, and disciplined" skew, not a broad recovery. Damodaran's S&P 500 revaluation at the new price level and rates still came out close to fairly valued ([Market Resilience or Investors In Denial?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/07/market-resilience-or-investors-in.html), 2023-07). By Q3 2023, the same concentration story continued (seven stocks — NVIDIA/Apple/Microsoft/Alphabet/Meta/Amazon/Tesla — accounted for $3.7 trillion of the $5.2 trillion 2023 increase in US equity value) while the ERP held nearly flat (4.84% on Oct 1 vs. 5.00% on Jul 1) even as the 10-year Treasury approached a 15-year high (4.59%), so the quarter's equity and bond losses traced to rising rates, not a repricing of risk; the risk-capital tracker (IPO counts, VC funding, high-yield issuance) still showed risk capital on the sidelines, because the equity recovery was concentrated in large, money-making companies rather than broadening into smaller, money-losing ones ([Market Bipolarity: Exuberance versus Exhaustion!](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/10/market-bipolarit-exuberance-versus.html), 2023-10).
