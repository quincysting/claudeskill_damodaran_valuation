## Alibaba
Pre-IPO (2014-05): dominant Chinese e-commerce (TaoBao/TMall, ~75% of online retail transactions), ~50% pre-tax margin, ~10% effective tax rate. Inputs: 27%→2.63% fading revenue growth, margin fading 50%→40%, sales/capital 2.0, cost of capital 8.84%→8%. Value: **$130.6B pre-proceeds / $145.6B incl. IPO proceeds ($61.46/share)**. Revisit (2014-09, post-prospectus update): value **$161B ($66/share)**, close to bankers' $60–66 range — more coincidence than consensus, since bankers price, not value. Relative-valuation exercise showed multiples/comparables could justify **$11.9B to $944B** depending on choices. Governance post (2014-09): Cayman-Islands VIE structure, board picked by Jack Ma's partnership, not shareholders. Applies the **"It Proposition"** (a factor only affects value if it changes cash flow or risk) to argue governance's effect should be modeled as an option-like premium/discount, not an ad hoc haircut; Monte Carlo range **$38–$153/share**. ([A China Story](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/05/alibaba-china-story-with-profitable.html) 2014-05; [Coming out party](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/09/alibabas-coming-out-party-valued-right.html) 2014-09; [Governance by Politburo](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/09/alibabas-governance-by-politburo.html) 2014-09)

**Lesson**: qualitative concerns (governance, brand, control) belong in a DCF only via an explicit cash-flow/risk channel; IPO bankers price to a target pop, not to value.

## Alphabet / Google
**Name change (2015-09)**: rebrand to "Alphabet" holding company. Cites academic studies showing stock prices jumped both when firms *added* ".com" in the 1990s and when they *removed* it after 2000 — pure pricing, no fundamentals. Diagnoses two real problems a genuine restructuring would need to fix: the "Sugar Daddy Syndrome" (moonshots funded without discipline by ad-cash flows) and founder "Benevolent Dictatorship" governance; proposes falsifiable tests (would Alphabet ever deny funding to a Page/Brin-favored project?). **Valuation duel vs. Apple (2016-02)**: narrative unchanged from Nov-2015 (12% revenue growth, margin raised to 32%) → **value $631/share** vs. price $682.40 (class C, no-vote) — ~8% overvalued, 35% chance undervalued. Flags that "Other Bets" generated only $448M revenue against $3,567M in losses, and that reported "operating income" was really EBITDA — undercutting Alphabet's own transparency rationale. Verdict: better *business* than Apple, worse *investment* at the price (Apple's breakeven needs zero growth; Google's needs sustained 12% growth at an already-high margin); better short-term *trade* on momentum. ([What's in a name?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/09/what-in-name-of-umlauts-alphabets-and.html) 2015-09; [Race to the top](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/02/race-to-top-duel-between-alphabet-and.html) 2016-02)

**Lesson**: name changes/reorgs are price events, not value events, unless tied to a specific, testable change in capital-allocation behavior; "which company is better" depends on whether you mean as a business, an investment, or a trade — three different answers.

## Amazon
**Field of Dreams (2014-10)**: Bezos's strategy — sell at/below cost to build market power, profit later. Shipping and Prime lose money by design. Recapitalizing tech spend raises current margin 2.09%→6.58% (2016 update). 2014 DCF: revenue to ~$250B by 2024, terminal margin 7.38% → **value $81B ($175/share)** vs. price $295; bull case (12.84% margin) → $345. Key finding: margin, not growth, is the value driver — below ~4% margin, more growth *destroys* value. **2016-02 update** (paired with Netflix): target margin 8.80% → **$323.55/share** vs. price $507 (still overvalued). **Netflix** (same post): narrative shifted to "global + original content" → **$61.44/share** vs. $87.40 (~40% overvalued). **Amazon Prime deconstruction (2017-10)**, using a user-based (not aggregated) method: 85M members, $99/yr US fee (modeled at a $93.78 global weighted average after Damodaran's correction), ~$1,300/yr Prime spend vs. $700 non-Prime, 96% renewal, $7.2B net shipping subsidy (~$120/member). Existing members worth **$486 each ($41.3B total)**; sensitivity shows if shipping-cost growth matched revenue growth, per-member value collapses to **$71.50** — the single biggest value lever. New members: **$52,792.78M** (negative if shipping costs aren't contained). Corporate media/content drag: **-$32.8B**. **Total Prime value ≈ $61.3B**. Reframes the $14B Whole Foods deal as buying shipping infrastructure (460 stores), not grocery-market entry. Post includes a rare public correction after reader feedback (international pricing, margin double-count fix, member drop-out rate). ([Field of Dreams](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/10/if-you-build-it-revenues-they-profits.html) 2014-10; [Disruptive Duo](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/02/the-disruptive-duo-amazon-and-netflix.html) 2016-02; [Deconstructing Prime](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/10/loss-leader-or-value-creator.html) 2017-10)

**Lesson**: treating multi-year R&D/content spend as an ordinary opex understates both margin and true reinvestment simultaneously; user-based and aggregate DCF should converge as cross-checks, and isolating one cost-growth assumption (e.g., shipping vs. revenue growth) can reveal whether a subscription program is value-creating or value-destroying, and can reframe M&A logic (Whole Foods) around that lever.

## Apple
Owned/traded repeatedly across the whole period as Damodaran's running "value vs. price" example.
- **2013-01**: after the fall from $705→$440, Monte Carlo (growth −2%→14%, margin 25–35%, WACC 11–14%) around a **$609** Dec-2012 DCF value → ~90% chance undervalued at $440; capped position at 10% of portfolio, no averaging down.
- **2013-02**: formalizes the value/price framework (magnitude of gap / confidence it closes / catalysts); DCF $608 vs. $440. Rebuts Einhorn's preferred-stock plan as creating **zero** value (no cash-flow/risk/WACC change) — at most a price catalyst (upside capped at ~$140/share, and only if the market were valuing Apple's cash at zero); a bond issue would add real (~$10/share) tax-shield value that preferred stock wouldn't.
- **2013-04**: post-earnings **$588/share** (growth 6%→5%, margin 30%→25%, WACC 12.49%→11.29% after $50B debt/buyback) vs. price $420; break-even needs revenue to *decline* 5%/yr. Judges "Delled" and "Microsofted" risk both low.
- **2013-09**: **$617/share**, reflects buyback-driven share-count decline.
- **2014-04 ("Watch the Gap")**: publishes the full 12-valuation history (Jan 2011 $385 vs price $339 → peak estimate $686 in Jul 2012 → low $595 in Apr 2013 vs price $385 → Apr 2014 ~$675 pre-earnings / $674 post-earnings after a share-count correction, originally $649/$648). Post-earnings stock jumped 13%+; Damodaran suspects the 7-for-1 **stock split** (zero theoretical value effect) drove the mood shift more than the $17B bond issue (~+$5/share value) or buyback increase — evidence corporate "value-unlocking" actions mostly move price, not value.
- **2015-08**: value $96 (Aug 2014) → $118 (Mar 2015, sold near $128) → $130 (Jul 2015, cash >$200B) → stock fell <$110, judged a buy.
- **2016-02 (vs. Alphabet)**: **$126/share** vs. price $94 (~25% undervalued, >90% probability per simulation).
- **2016-06 (Icahn/Buffett)**: builds a taxonomy — insider/activist trades can rationally shift your value estimate; a trader's trade shifts only price/momentum; another value investor's trade is a catalyst, not new information. Classifies Icahn as a trader ("casual pricing": EPS×P/E+cash, not DCF), not a value investor; Buffett's IBM-style mature-tech pattern reinforces Apple as low-growth/cash-rich. Neither changed Damodaran's own estimate.
- **2017-02 ("Greatest Cash Machine")**: cash balance ~$250B; FCFE $93.6B in 2015 alone; $183B returned to shareholders 2013–2016 (yet cash still grew). Sets an explicit **sell trigger at $140/share**. Publishes full 2010–2017 price-vs-value chart; credits luck as much as (or more than) insight.

**Lesson**: a large, high-confidence value/price gap doesn't justify an oversized bet because convergence timing isn't controllable; buy-below/sell-above is internally consistent even on a short holding period — "buy and hold forever" isn't required by value investing; corporate cash-return/split announcements move price via psychology far more than via any calculable value effect; classify a celebrity investor's type (insider/activist/trader/value-investor) before letting their trade move your own view.

Sources: [Are you a value investor?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/01/are-you-value-investor-apple-test.html) 2013-01; [Market Mayhem](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/01/market-mayhem-lessons-for-apple.html) 2013-01; [Back to Apple](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/02/apple-redux-thoughts-on-value-price-and.html) 2013-02; [Financial Alchemy](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/02/financial-alchemy-david-einhorns-value.html) 2013-02; [News, Noise and Value](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/04/apple-calm-after-storm.html) 2013-04; [Love the company!](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/09/love-company-love-product-love-stock.html) 2013-09; [Watch the Gap](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/04/watch-gap-apples-long-and-twisted.html) 2014-04; [Narrative Resets](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/narrative-resets-revisiting-tech-trio.html) 2015-08; [Race to the top](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/02/race-to-top-duel-between-alphabet-and.html) 2016-02; [Icahn exits, Buffett enters](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/06/icahn-buffett-and-apple-big-name.html) 2016-06; [Greatest Cash Machine](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/02/apple-greatest-cash-machine-in-history.html) 2017-02

## Blackberry
**2013-09 ("Decline and Denial")**: life-cycle framework (born→grow→mature→decline). Dec-2011 valued at ~$9B assuming a niche "Blackberry Boring" corporate-security product wound down over 5 years. By Sept 2013, Fairfax Financial offered to take it private for **$4.7B**; skeptical the liquidation plan can still work given value already destroyed. **2014-09 ("Walking Dead")**: revenue $18.4B (FY2011-12)→$4.1B (TTM), operating income +$1.85B→-$2.7B loss; market cap $5.3B vs. Apple's $163B/Google's $63B cash (either could "buy it with petty cash"). Defines the **"Walking Dead" company** archetype: broken model + management denial + an enabling ecosystem of consultants/bankers + enough residual cash to keep destroying value slowly. ([Decline and Denial](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/09/decline-and-denial-requiem-for.html) 2013-09; [Walking Dead](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/09/the-walking-dead-blackberry-yahoo-and.html) 2014-09)

**Lesson**: declining firms should be valued/run for orderly harvest and wind-down, not growth comeback; delay in accepting decline destroys liquidation value.

## Deutsche Bank
**2016-10**: after a proposed $14B DOJ mortgage-fine, stock fell to $13.33 (mkt cap $17.99B, down >80% from pre-2008). Bank-specific DCF method: (1) ROE path from -13.70% (base year) climbing to the 25th-percentile bank ROE (5.85%, yr 5) then to cost of equity (9.44%, yr 10); (2) **regulatory capital as the reinvestment driver** — FCFE = Net Income − change in required Tier 1 capital, modeled rising from 12.41% to the sector 75th percentile (15.67%), explicitly pricing in future dilution; (3) cost of equity backed out from the sector's own Price/Book-vs-ROE relationship (median bank: 9.44%; 25th/75th percentile of risk: 7.76%/10.20%) — Deutsche starts at 10.20% and moves to 9.44% by year 10. Result: **$22.97/share**, then cut for a 10% catastrophic-wipeout probability → **$20.67/share final** (~36% undervalued at $13.33). Decomposition from a $33.63 unadjusted value: dilution effect -$10.66/share (-31.69%), wipeout risk -$2.30 (-6.83%). Bought the stock, citing a mixed-but-net-positive track record buying into scandals (JPMorgan 2012, VW 2015 worked; Valeant hadn't yet). ([Greek Tragedy](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/10/deutsche-bank-greek-tragedy-at-german.html) 2016-10)

**Lesson**: for banks, model reinvestment as required regulatory-capital changes (not capex), and derive cost of equity from the sector's own P/B-ROE relationship as a model-agnostic cross-check.

## Dell
**2013-02**: Michael Dell (14% owner, CEO) proposes taking Dell private at **$13.65/share** (~$24B, ~$15B debt). Frames management buyouts as a structural conflict (fiduciary duty to get the highest price vs. buyer's incentive to pay the lowest). Rebuts the "fair value" bank-appraisal defense, the "market is right, we're paying a premium" defense, and the "open to other bidders" defense. Proposes charging management a cost-of-equity penalty for the >$7B spent on failed prior acquisitions before allowing a cheap buyout — the approach Southeastern Asset Management (>7% holder) used to value Dell at **$23.72/share**. Damodaran's own DCF: **$16.38/share** — above the offer, below Southeastern's number; Michael Dell "probably getting a bargain, but not a huge one." ([Conflicted Buyout](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/02/michael-dells-conflicted-buyout.html) 2013-02)

**Lesson**: bank "fairness opinions" paid by the buyout group are not neutral; charging management a capital-cost penalty for prior value destruction is a legitimate valuation correction in an MBO.

## Facebook
**2013-07**: history — $71B/$28-share pre-IPO "Google wannabe" narrative → declined $38 IPO price → bought at $18 (Sept 2012) → by Jul 2013, after 3 earnings beats, value **$27.65/share** vs. price ~$38 → **sold**. **2015-08 ("Narrative Resets")**: narrative broadened (could exceed Google in ad monetization) → Aug-2014 value ~$63 vs. price $72 (overpriced); by Jul 2015, with Instagram ($600M→~$3B ad revenue trajectory), value **$69** vs. price **$95** (still too rich). **2016-02 ("Management Matters")**: post-earnings (44% growth, 51.36% margin) narrative shifted to Facebook as the likely outright *winner* over Google → value **~$95** vs. price **$102** (fairly priced). Credits the mobile pivot and the WhatsApp deal (bought 450M users at ~$40/user vs. market's ~$100/user pricing at the time) as vindicated pricing bets. **2014-02 ("Facebook buys Whatsapp for $19 billion")**: analyzed the $19B deal ($15B stock + $4B cash) on both value and pricing axes. *Value*: break-even pre-tax income needed to justify $19B ranges from **$2.17B** (8% required return, immediate) to **$4.371B** (10% return, 5-year wait) — implausible under WhatsApp's then-existing $1/year-after-free model without ~2.5B users. *Pricing*: a cross-company correlation analysis found number of users, not revenue/earnings, was the dominant driver of social-media market caps; at Facebook's own $130/user market pricing, WhatsApp's 450M users (valuing only the ~160M incremental to Facebook's ecosystem) implied **~$20.8B** — higher than the price paid, making the deal defensible as pricing even though it couldn't be justified as value. ([Facebook buys Whatsapp](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/02/facebook-buys-whatsapp-for-19-billion.html) 2014-02) Notes a **postscript warning**: Google's and Facebook's combined implied 10-year revenue need (~$300B, ~75% of the entire projected online-ad market) makes holding *both* simultaneously arithmetically hard to justify. ([Market Mood Swings](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/07/facebook-hits-ipo-price-mood-swings-in.html) 2013-07; [Narrative Resets](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/narrative-resets-revisiting-tech-trio.html) 2015-08; [Management Matters](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/02/management-matters-facebook-and-twitter.html) 2016-02)

**Lesson**: when several holdings compete for the same finite TAM, sum their implied revenue requirements before holding more than one; a "narrative lag" (not updating your model fast enough as a company executes) is itself a source of investing error, in both directions (sold FB too soon; see Twitter for the mirror case).

## Ferrari
**2015-10**: pre-IPO spinoff from Fiat. Auto industry framed as structurally bad (5.63% revenue CAGR 2005–2014, thin margins). Ferrari sold only 7,255 cars/year, 18.20% pre-tax margin (vs. 6.58% sector), 14.56% ROC (vs. 6.68%). Two narratives modeled: **"Status Quo"** (stay exclusive) → **€6,310M (~$7B)**; **"Rev It Up"** (broaden with a cheaper model, doubling volume) → **€6,042M (~$6.75B)** — *lower*, because volume growth costs more margin than it adds. Explicitly rejects adding a separate "brand premium": it's already embedded in the elevated margin/ROC assumptions (double-counting otherwise). Status-quo value ≈ **€31.5/share** if the rumored 200M shares are created, vs. rumored pricing of €50/share (€10B equity) and a push for an €11B valuation. ([The Ferrari IPO](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/10/the-ferrari-ipo-price-premium-for.html) 2015-10)

**Lesson**: a brand premium must already live in the margin/ROC assumptions, never as a bolt-on; for a scarcity-based luxury business, "growing the volume" can destroy value relative to staying small.

## General Motors
**2015-03**: rebuts criticism of a ~$5B buyback as harmful. Traces GM's decline from 1920s disruptor to 2008 bailout (taxpayers lost $11.2B). Post-2010 "new GM": $25.5B reinvested 2010–2014, revenue +3.56%/yr, but **ROC (6.86% even in the best year) lagged WACC throughout** — i.e., that reinvestment was value-destructive. Industry-wide: global auto ROC (6.47%) trailed WACC (7.53%); only Audi, among the ten largest automakers, cleared its cost of capital in 2014. Conclusion: in a sector where even the best incumbents can't earn their cost of capital, returning cash via buyback is the value-maximizing choice, not short-termism; frames the real dispute as philosophical (shareholder-return mission vs. output/employment mission), not financial. ([The GM Buyback](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/03/the-gm-buyback-beyond-hysteria.html) 2015-03)

**Lesson**: judge a buyback against what the alternative use of that cash would actually earn, not against the buyback in isolation.

## GoPro / LinkedIn
**2016-02**: **GoPro** — original Oct-2014 valuation $30.57 (36%/yr growth to ~$10B revenue, 12.5% margin); after a weak report crashed the stock to $9.78, revised down (revenue $4.7B, margin 9.84%, 20% failure risk) → **$17.66/share**, 68% probability undervalued at $12.81. **LinkedIn** — ~80% subscription/recruiting revenue (not ad-based), GAAP-profitable 2013–14. Revalued at 25%/yr growth to $15.3B revenue, 18% margin → **$103.49/share** vs. price (~10% overvalued, 40% probability undervalued). Acquisition-floor exercise: modeling a buyout floor at $8B had surprisingly small effect on the value distribution, because $8B sits well below the $15.4B market cap and the assumed buyout price itself falls in the same bad-growth/bad-margin scenarios where the floor would be needed. Explicitly discloses rooting for LinkedIn over GoPro going in — the model didn't validate that bias (GoPro came out cheap, LinkedIn fair-priced). ([Lazarus Rising or Icarus Falling?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/02/lazarus-rising-or-icarus-falling-gopro.html) 2016-02)

**Lesson**: an "acquisition floor" provides less protection than intuition suggests because the assumed buyout price falls together with fundamentals; disclose your bias before valuing, then check whether the output actually diverged from it.

## HP
**2014-10**: HP's planned break-up into PCs/printers vs. business services, under CEO Meg Whitman (who had earlier rejected the same plan). HP's own deck implied **$2.8B** of "corporate cost" would vanish post-split. Sum-of-parts value **+$31.85B** over consolidated — but modeled as scaling almost entirely with how much of that $2.8B in savings is real ($0 saved → $0 value gained; $2.5B/yr saved → $27,738M; $3B/yr saved → $34,522M). Rejects every standard rationale for needing a split to realize the savings (same management runs both halves; no culture break, since both keep the HP name; no regulatory constraint; no debt-capacity unlock). ([HP's Break Up](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/10/hps-break-up-value-enhancement-pricing.html) 2014-10)

**Lesson**: isolate what a breakup specifically requires that the combined company couldn't do anyway — if the "value creation" evaporates once you strip out an assumed synergy achievable without splitting, the breakup is pricing theater.

## Lyft
**2015-10**: narrower "US-only, ride-sharing-only" narrative vs. Uber. 2015 gross billings $1.2B (140% growth) vs. Uber's $10.84B (442%). Inputs: US-only ~$150B TAM, 25% market share (national, not global, network effect), 25% margin, 15% revenue slice, 12% WACC, 10% failure risk → **value $3.1B** — <1/7 of Uber's contemporaneous $23.4B. Pricing cross-check across 6 ride-sharing firms: Lyft (priced at **$2.5B**) looked cheap on every multiple vs. Uber (priced at **$51B**) looking expensive on each — e.g., Value/Gross Billing 2.08x vs. 4.70x; Uber's own escalating VC price appeared to drag up peers' prices on a shared "pricing ladder." Conclusion: at contemporaneous prices, Lyft (undervalued) was the better investment despite Uber's larger absolute value and price. ([Dream Big or Stay Focused?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/10/dream-big-or-stay-focused-lyfts-counter.html) 2015-10)

**Lesson**: track value and price separately for each company in a hot sector — a shared "pricing ladder" can drag up peer valuations regardless of their own fundamentals.

## Microsoft
**2013-09** (same "Decline and Denial" post as Blackberry): the archetypal **value trap** — cheap on every multiple for a decade, poor investment because management kept reinvesting in low-return "growth." FY2013: pre-tax operating income $26,764M/revenue $77,849M (income -4.26% YoY despite revenue +5.6%); effective tax rate 19.2%, WACC assumed 8%. **Zero-growth value of assets in place** = $270,316M; + cash $77,022M − debt $15,600M = **$331,738M equity value, ~22% above** the $270,540M market cap — i.e., Microsoft was worth *more* assuming no growth at all than the market paid, because assumed ~6% ROC on new ventures fell below the 8% cost of capital, making continued "growth-seeking" value-destructive. Ties this directly to the market's negative reaction to the Nokia acquisition (-$15B market cap). ([Decline and Denial](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/09/decline-and-denial-requiem-for.html) 2013-09)

**Lesson**: a "cheap" mature company stays cheap as long as management keeps reinvesting below its cost of capital; the market wants capital discipline (dividends/buybacks), not more acquisitions.

## Petrobras
**2015-02**: state-controlled Brazilian oil major, dual-class shares (government controls >50% of voting shares + a golden veto), lost **~$200B in market cap** from its 2010 peak of ~$244B (~$160B of that *before* the 2014 oil-price crash). Damodaran's 5-step "roadmap for value destruction": over-invest without regard to returns (capex/exploration >35% of revenue vs. 15–20% sector norm, ROC fell to 5% even with $100+ oil); grow revenue regardless of profitability (aided by mandated subsidized gasoline pricing); pay dividends "like a regulated utility" it isn't; borrow to cover the resulting deficit ($135B debt, most of any oil major); value destroyed. Regressed revenue on oil price (R²=92%) to normalize for the crash: at $51.69 oil, normalized revenue $61.3B × trailing 10.82% operating margin → DCF value **$62.4B**, below the **$135.1B debt** — equity effectively worthless in the base case. The most optimistic scenario (revenue held at $135.8B, 21.1% margin, 13.36% ROC, 43.5% debt ratio — assumptions that "border on fantasy") yields **$8.11/share (R$22.55)**, well above the market price of $3.28 (R$9.12). ([Petrobras Limbo](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/02/how-low-can-you-go-doing-petrobras-limbo.html) 2015-02)

**Lesson**: for a politically-controlled company, regress the operating driver against the relevant commodity/macro price to normalize a cyclical collapse, then check whether even an optimistic turnaround clears the debt load before concluding there's any equity value; dual-class structures that separate ownership from control are a recurring enabler of this kind of destruction.

## Pfizer / Allergan
**2015-11**: proposed tax-inversion merger. Separates "buying growth" (Allergan's 40.62%/yr revenue growth vs. Pfizer's -6%/yr) from "saving taxes." Growth argument rejected: Allergan already traded at large premiums (EV/EBIT 53.15 vs. sector 20.12) before the bid, so the growth was already priced in, and its accounting resembled Valeant's. Tax mechanics: pre-2017 US law's ~40% marginal rate, worldwide (not territorial) taxation, and the repatriation-deferral trigger (>$2T trapped offshore industry-wide) make inversion rational; Pfizer's own effective rate was 23.45% vs. 40% marginal, with ~$12B of its $20.66B cash trapped. Three-scenario DCF ("Patriot Games" repatriate now / "Wait-it-out" defer / "Go Irish" invert): inversion raises equity value from **$176,047M ($28.53/share)** under Wait-it-out to **$209,637M ($34.39/share)** — a **$33,590M ($5.86/share)** benefit on an R&D-expensed basis; with R&D capitalized (the headline figure) the benefit is $27,153M ($4.69/share). Concludes the deal is likely value-*negative* net of a rumored 30% premium (>$30B over Allergan's market cap), absent demonstrated synergy. ([Value and Taxes](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/11/value-and-taxes-breaking-down-pfizer.html) 2015-11)

**Lesson**: value a deal's growth rationale and its tax rationale separately — "buying growth" only creates value if the target is priced at a discount to that growth, rarely true for an already-hot grower.

## Snap
**2017-02**: pre-IPO. Rejects the prospectus's "camera company" framing — it's an online-ad business. TAM context: digital ad spend ~$200B (2016)→>$300B (2020 est.); Google+Facebook already ~43% of the market and ~2/3 of its recent growth (another instance of the "big market delusion" pattern). Benchmarked against Google/LinkedIn/Facebook/Twitter at their own IPOs: Snap's IPO-year revenue ($405M) exceeded only LinkedIn's and its operating loss (-$521M) was the largest of the group, but engagement (25 min/day) trailed only Facebook and Google (50 each, Google incl. YouTube). Narrative: pure-advertising, youth-focused, engagement-over-user-count. Inputs: target operating margin close to Google's (~25%), not Facebook's (~45%), WACC ~10%, 10% failure probability → **base case $14.4B ($11/share)**; simulation median $13.3B, range from <0 to **$66B ($50/share)**, spanning named scenarios "Facebook Light" (~$48B) vs. "Twitter Redux" (~$4B). Bankers' reported IPO range ($18.5–21.5B) exceeded Damodaran's estimate — treated as pricing, not a refutation of value. ([My Snap Story](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/02/a-snap-story-valuing-snap-ahead-of-its.html) 2017-02)

**Lesson**: benchmark a pre-IPO company against several different prior IPOs on the same metrics, since it rarely cleanly matches one comparable; name explicit bull/bear narrative endpoints to make a wide valuation range interpretable as competing stories, not model noise.

## Tesla
- **2013-09**: stock $168.76 (~$20B mkt cap) vs. $1.33B revenue, -$217M operating loss. Modeled as an automobile company reaching Audi-scale revenue (~$64B) at a 12.5% margin (95th-percentile auto/near-tech), 10.03% WACC (60/40 auto/tech blend), 10% failure risk → **$67.12/share**, far below market; <10% probability value exceeds price. Follow-up post walks through three bull narratives Damodaran doesn't personally buy (disruptor, battery/OEM supplier, network winner) plus a strategic-acquirer scenario.
- **2014-03 ("Return to the firing line")**: first revisit of the September 2013 $67.12 estimate, with the stock now at $220. Updated inputs from the FY2013 10-K: base revenue $2,014M (up from $1,329M), year-10 revenue raised to $79,215M, margin target trimmed slightly to 12% (adding a battery/Gigafactory business lowers blended margin even as it raises revenue), sales/capital improved to 1.55, WACC down to 8.86% (lower ERP, more auto/less tech weighting), and probability of failure cut to 0% (helped by a $2B convertible-bond raise). Result: equity value **$16,681M**, or **$99.85–$118.47/share** depending on how employee options are valued (~$110–115 blended) — equity value up 105% on the September 2013 estimate, but still only about half the market price. Frames this as evidence that intrinsic value for a young company can legitimately double in nine months without contradicting an earlier "overvalued" call, since both narrative and fundamentals had genuinely improved. ([Return to the firing line](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/03/return-to-firing-line-revisiting-tesla.html) 2014-03)
- **2016-07 ("story stock")**: introduces the "story stock" concept — narrative dominates price/value, and disagreement is best organized along 5 axes (business identity, market focus, competitive edge, investment intensity, risk). Valuation trajectory: Sept 2013 **$70/share** (pure auto story) → July 2015 **$123/share** (Gigafactory shifts story toward tech) → July 2016 **$151/share** (Model 3's ~400K pre-orders shift story toward mass market) — vs. market caps of $28B (2013) and $33B (both 2015 and 2016). Flags the concurrent SolarCity acquisition as narrative-muddying, and Musk's temperament (compared to "first-iteration" Steve Jobs) as a specific execution risk — can he shift from visionary to operational builder?
- **2017-08**: stock at $365 (passed Ford/GM in market cap). Revenue $10.07B TTM (+107% annualized); raised 10-yr revenue target to ~$93B → **value $192/share**, still far below price. Names three execution tests: sell ~2M cars/yr by 2027, actually manufacture at that scale/cost, sustain a 12% margin (vs. 6.33% sector). Separately critiques Tesla's **debt puzzle**: planning a $1.5B junk-bond issue at a hoped-for 5.25% while carrying ~$4.3B in NOLs that neutralize debt's tax benefit for ~7 years — the classic debt-tax-benefit-vs-cost trade-off is maximally lopsided against debt for a young, loss-making, founder-controlled grower; Damodaran attributes the choice of debt over a stock issue to a "dilution bogeyman" and banker inertia.

([Tesla Test](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/09/valuation-of-week-1-tesla-test.html) 2013-09; [Follow up](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/09/tesla-follow-up.html) 2013-09; [Return to the firing line](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/03/return-to-firing-line-revisiting-tesla.html) 2014-03; [Story stock](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/07/tesla-story-meets-numbers-promise-meets.html) 2016-07; [2017 Update](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/08/a-tesla-2017-update-disruptive-force.html) 2017-08)

**Lesson**: for a story stock, organize disagreement as an explicit menu of narrative choices, not discount-rate haggling; evaluate a company's debt-vs-equity choice against its *actual* tax position and life-cycle stage — a lossmaking high-growth firm with large NOLs gets none of debt's benefits while bearing all its costs.

## Theranos
**2015-11**: not a DCF (private, opaque) but a red-flag case study. $9B+ VC valuation on a blood-testing device (Nanotainer) that a WSJ investigation found wasn't reliable/wasn't actually used for most tests; FDA restricted it to one approved test. Three named red flags: **"the runaway story"** (an emotionally compelling narrative suppresses scrutiny), **"the black turtleneck"** (cultivated founder-cult imagery substituting for substance), and **governance failure even at private companies** — a 12-person board stacked with former cabinet/military officials (Kissinger, Shultz, Mattis) with no diagnostics/FDA expertise, plus a Dec-2013 request for a 5-for-1 split and dual-class shares consolidating Holmes's control. ([Runaway Stories](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/11/runaway-stories-and-fairy-tale-endings.html) 2015-11)

**Lesson**: governance diligence applies to private/VC-backed companies too — a board of prestigious names with no domain expertise in the company's actual technical/regulatory risk is a red flag, not reassurance.

## Twitter
- **2013-09 (pricing)**: pre-financials IPO pricing exercise (not valuation) — extrapolated private-transaction prices ($100M 2008→$10.5B 2013) and revenue/user multiples to guess a **$10–15B** offering (predicted ~$12B).
- **2013-10 (valuation)**: post-S-1, DCF with 55%/yr→2.7% fading growth to $11.5B revenue by 2023, 25% terminal margin (vs. Facebook's 30%), beta-derived WACC 11.22% → **$17.36/share ($9.97B)**; sets Damodaran's own tiers ($10/share = very good deal, $17.5 = indifferent, $35 = moon shot). Companion post formalizes the "Pricing Game vs. Value Game" table (philosophy, drivers, tools, time horizon, danger — for traders vs. investors).
- **2013-10 (IPO end-game)**: offering range set at $17–20 (low end of market expectations), which Damodaran guesses is ~20% below the bankers' own view of fair price (~$25), set to engineer a first-day pop; the valuation, updated for the amended filings, rises only to **$17.84/share**.
- **2014-02**: post-earnings (stock dropped 25% despite revenue +116% YoY) update to **$20.57/share** vs. price $53 — illustrates incremental/trivial news moving price sharply in a "pricer's game."
- **2014-11 ("Bar Mitzvah")**: argues social media is transitioning from narrative-priced to numbers-priced; using Twitter's own 10-yr revenue guidance (~$14B) in the model gives **$20.81/share** vs. price $41.85 — Twitter would need $30–40B revenue to justify the price.
- **2015-08 / 2016-02**: value stayed **~$18–26/share** across 2015–16 vs. prices from $27 down to an all-time-low $14.31; bought at $27 (2015), underwater by 2016. Diagnoses management chasing analyst-pleasing user-growth metrics over monetization; the right-skewed (option-like) simulated value distribution was the basis for treating it as a fair-value-but-optionality bet even as fundamentals disappointed. Notes a postscript: Google's and Facebook's combined implied 10-year revenue need (~$300B, ~75% of the entire projected online-ad market) makes holding both, plus smaller names like Twitter, arithmetically hard to justify simultaneously.

([Pricing Game](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/09/twitter-announces-ipo-pricing-game.html) 2013-09; [The Valuation](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/10/twitter-announces-ipo-valuation.html) 2013-10; [Good trade, bad investment](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/10/twitter-ipo-why-good-trade-be-bad.html) 2013-10; [IPO End Game](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/10/the-twitter-ipo-thoughts-on-ipo-end-game.html) 2013-10; [Incremental News](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/02/incremental-news-price-and-value.html) 2014-02; [Bar Mitzvah](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/11/twitter-bar-mitzvah-is-social-media.html) 2014-11; [Narrative Resets](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/08/narrative-resets-revisiting-tech-trio.html) 2015-08; [Management Matters](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/02/management-matters-facebook-and-twitter.html) 2016-02)

**Lesson**: IPO bankers price (to engineer a first-day pop), they don't value; a sector-wide shift from narrative-pricing to numbers-pricing ("bar mitzvah") changes which metrics the market rewards; management that optimizes for analyst-favored metrics (users) over monetization can keep "growing" while destroying investment value; when several companies compete for one finite TAM, sum their implied revenue needs before holding more than one.

## Uber
- **2014-06**: first valuation, reacting to a $17B VC round. Modeled as urban car-service only: $100B TAM growing 6%/yr, 10% terminal share, 20% revenue slice, 40% terminal margin, 5.0 sales/capital, WACC 12%→8%, 10% failure risk → **$5.895B** vs. implied $17B. Introduces a real-options framing for "disruption" — justifying $17B would need a total market ~3x Damodaran's estimate (~$300B) or more than double the 10% market share.
- **2014-12 (crowd valuation)**: turns the model into an explicit 6-step narrative-choice menu (potential market $100B–$285B / market growth 3–10.4% / share 5–40% via network-effect strength / revenue slice 5–20% / sales-capital 1.5–10 / WACC 7–12%) — range **$799M to $90.5B**, demonstrating that DCF isn't inherently "too conservative" for young disruptive companies; big values require big, explicit narrative assumptions.
- **2015-10 ("Rollercoaster")**: incorporating a year of evidence (leaked $10.84B gross billings, driver-employment rulings, weak city-level contribution margins of 3.5–11.1%, losing ground to local rivals in Asia) → **$23.4B**, vs. VC pricing then ~$51B.
- **2015-10 (vs. Lyft)**: Lyft's narrower US-only story values it at just **$3.1B** (vs. Uber's $23.4B) but Lyft was priced *cheaper relative to value* ($2.5B price vs Uber's $51B price) — better investment despite being the smaller company.
- **2017-06 ("bad week")**: amid Kalanick's resignation and a string of scandals, applies a **story break / story change / story shift** framework — concludes this is a "story shift" (narrative direction intact, contours adjusted), not a break; value held at **~$25B operating + $11B cash/Didi stake**, with stakeholder-by-stakeholder analysis (customers, drivers, regulators, engineers, investors) showing most groups have too much at stake to abandon Uber over ethics headlines, except engineering talent (female engineering share reportedly fell 25%→<3%).
- **2017-06 (user-based cross-check)**: builds a disaggregated per-user valuation (existing users worth ~$410 each based on $50.50/yr profit-per-user, 15-yr lifetime, 95% renewal; new users add $20.2B net of ~$240 acquisition cost; corporate drag -$10.4B) → **$26.2B operating value**, converging closely with the aggregated DCF (~$25B) — plus cash/Didi stake to **$37.2B** total equity. Warns that naive "price per user" comparisons across ride-sharing peers are misleading since they blend existing- and future-user value.

([Cab Ride to Riches](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html) 2014-06; [Crowd valuation](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/12/up-up-and-away-crowd-valuation-of-uber.html) 2014-12; [Rollercoaster](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/10/on-uber-rollercoaster-narrative-tweaks.html) 2015-10; [vs. Lyft](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/10/dream-big-or-stay-focused-lyfts-counter.html) 2015-10; [Bad week](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/06/ubers-bad-week-doomsday-scenario-or.html) 2017-06; [User Economics](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/06/usersubscriber-economics-alternative.html) 2017-06)

**Lesson**: most of the "error" in valuing a young disruptor lives in the narrative, not the discount-rate mechanics — track how the story (not the model) changes over time; when scandal hits a story stock, trace the causal mechanism through each dependent stakeholder group rather than reacting to headlines; disaggregated (user-based) and aggregated DCF should converge as a sanity check.

## Vale
**2015-09**: third entry in a series that began with the Nov-2014 "go where it is darkest" post (Vale and Lukoil as commodity/currency/country-risk plays). Nov-2014: $19.40/share (iron-ore/country-risk stabilize) vs. price $8.53 — bought. Apr-2015: earnings fell another third, removed the assumed perpetual excess-return premium → $10.71 vs. $6.15 — held. ## Valeant
Four-post series tracking a losing position from "star" to "dog."
- **2015-11**: business model = acquire (debt-funded, $26.4B since 2010) → reprice "underpriced" drugs → minimize R&D → convert to earnings fast, aided by acquisition-related tax credits (effective rate <10%). Popular with Sequoia/Ackman. Short-seller (Citron) allegations of a hidden captive-pharmacy relationship crashed the stock >60% from its August peak. Valued as a conventional pharma company reverting to R&D-driven low growth (3%, margin cut 28.3%→25.5%, WACC 7.52%) → **$72.10/share** vs. price $83.64 (raised to $77–81 in an update adding Salix's missing quarters) — at best fairly valued, stayed on sidelines. Explicitly distinguishes Valeant (real assets, broken *strategy*) from Enron (fabricated assets).
- **2016-04**: uses the BCG matrix (star→cash cow→dog) to show the classification changing within a year. "Cash cow" value (Nov-2015 vintage): $77 vs. price $82. By April 2016: 6 months with no financial filings (risking covenant default), CEO hospitalized, Philidor ties deeper than disclosed, $30B+ debt, Moody's cut to junk (B1). Deliberately valued *before* the delayed 10-K, raising WACC to 8.29%, adding a 5% forced-liquidation risk (25% discount), assuming ~2% revenue restatement and half of acquisition expenses reclassified back to opex (pre-tax margin 43.66%→40.39%) → **$43.66/share** vs. price $32 — bought (in May 2016, at $27), explicitly betting on what the filing would show.
- **2016-11**: after a bad Q3 report, decomposed the operating-income decline and found **87%** of it was non-cash amortization/impairment catch-up on old acquisitions, not fresh deterioration — confirming rather than breaking the "dog" thesis. Updated value **$32.50** vs. price ~$15 (now *more* undervalued %-wise) — **doubled** the position, using an explicit "faith vs. feedback" framework (update the estimate on new information = feedback; act consistently with the updated estimate = faith).
- **2017-03**: FY2016 10-K showed further deterioration (revenue -7.35%, operating income -31.75%). Confronts that the story itself may now be *broken*, not just discounted — reputational damage may permanently block the "return to boring pharma" transition (researchers wary, price hikes scrutinized, buyers avoiding tainted assets). Ackman sold his stake at ~90% loss; doesn't follow, speculating Ackman's board veto may have actually blocked asset sales. Three scenarios: going concern (**$13.68/share**, 12% chance of equity going to zero) / acquisition target (dismissed) / sum-of-parts liquidation (needs to exceed ~$29B debt). Concludes modestly undervalued and holds — while candidly flagging this might be loss-aversion bias rather than a clean value judgment.

([Checkmate or Stalemate](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/11/checkmate-or-stalemate-valeants-fall.html) 2015-11; [Information Vacuums](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/04/valeant-information-vacuums-management.html) 2016-04; [Faith, Feedback and Fear](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/11/faith-feedback-and-fear-returning-to.html) 2016-11; [Damaged Goods](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/03/a-valeant-update-damaged-goods-or.html) 2017-03)

**Lesson**: when a position moves against you, decompose the bad news — a loss driven by non-cash impairment catch-up confirms a "damaged but not worsening" thesis, while fresh revenue/margin deterioration would refute it; when a company stops filing financials, waiting for the information isn't risk-free (the market usually re-prices faster than you can react), so an explicit, cautious provisional valuation is often better than waiting; unethical-but-legal conduct can permanently impair a company's ability to execute even a sound recovery plan, because counterparties rationally discount its future conduct.

## Volkswagen
**2015-10**: diesel-emissions "defeat device" scandal, stock down ~40% in a month. Two-step method: (1) solved for the operating income the pre-scandal market price implied (€7,683M, well below the trailing €12,886M and ~25% below the 5-year average — market was already discounting VW as a low-growth mature auto firm); (2) layered in deliberately conservative scandal costs — government fines (max EPA $18B/€16.07B + €10B assumed European pile-on = €26.07B), recall costs ~$1.6B (scaled from Toyota's 2010 recall) and legal costs ~$2.4B (matching the largest shareholder-suit judgment), only half of fines/legal costs tax-deductible, reputational lost-sales (20% of operating income for 5 years, PV $5.17B). Chose voting ("ordinary") over cheaper preferred shares, since a governance-driven scandal raises the value of having a voice. Bought the stock. ([Sturm und Drang](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/10/putting-price-tag-on-scandal-sturm-und.html) 2015-10)

**Lesson**: for a scandal, back out the market's implied pre-event operating assumptions first, then layer in deliberately conservative, individually justified cost line items with an outside reference case for scale (here, Toyota's 2010 recall).

## Yahoo
**2014-05 ("puzzle, mystery, enigma")**: sum-of-the-parts — declining US core (intrinsic $7.37B / relative $5.9B), thriving 35%-owned Yahoo Japan ($21B intrinsic / $34.2B relative), and 22.1%-owned pre-IPO Alibaba ($145B intrinsic / $117,623M relative). Netting capital-gains tax on Alibaba shares Yahoo was contractually obligated to sell: all-intrinsic roll-up **$41.19/share** (18% undervalued vs. $33.76 price); all-relative **$39.19** (14% undervalued). A third "pricing breakeven" check shows the market was implying a *negative* value for Yahoo's own core business at a $150B assumed Alibaba value. **2014-09 ("Walking Dead")**: post-Alibaba-IPO update — market-based sum-of-parts gives **$52.14/share** vs. price $40.66, implying **-$11.48/share (-$12.22B)** for Yahoo's own operating assets — a "Walking Dead discount" reflecting fear management will waste the cash windfall (full liquidation would trigger a $16.3B tax bill, cutting per-share value to $34.18). **2015-12 ("End Game")**: as the board considered selling the core business, updated sum-of-parts (Yahoo Japan $8,365M + Alibaba $31,955M + cash $5,882M − debt $2,161M + $0 for operating assets) = **$44,041M equity vs. $32,390M market cap** — a 28.9% discount to pure holding-company value. Argues CEO Marissa Mayer's turnaround mandate was doomed from the start since she controlled only the small "farmhouse" (US ops), not the valuable "farm" (cross-holdings); a declining company needs a pragmatic liquidator, not a visionary bold bet. ([Puzzle, mystery, enigma](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/05/yahoo-puzzle-mystery-and-enigma.html) 2014-05; [Walking Dead](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/09/the-walking-dead-blackberry-yahoo-and.html) 2014-09; [End Game](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/12/the-yahoo-chronicles-is-this-end-game.html) 2015-12)

**Lesson**: enterprise-value multiples applied to a parent's own reported financials silently assign zero value to non-consolidated cross-holdings; a negative implied value for a company's core operating assets (backed out from cross-holding market prices) is a specific red flag distinct from an ordinary "value trap" — it signals the market pricing in expected value destruction by management, not just underappreciated assets.

## Cross-case lessons
1. **Value vs. price is the master framework.** Across Apple, Tesla, Facebook, and Twitter, Damodaran repeatedly separates a DCF-driven "value" (cash flow, growth, risk) from a demand/supply-driven "price" (momentum, mood, liquidity); a confident value/price gap doesn't justify a large bet because the *timing* of convergence isn't controllable.
2. **IPOs and bank valuations are pricing exercises, not valuations.** Twitter, Alibaba, Snap: bankers price IPOs to engineer a first-day pop, deliberately under-pricing (by about 10–15% on average; ~20% in Damodaran's Twitter guess) ([IPO End Game](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/10/the-twitter-ipo-thoughts-on-ipo-end-game.html), 2013-10); a banker's number matching an independent valuation is more coincidence than consensus.
3. **Corporate actions marketed as "unlocking value" (buybacks, dividends, splits, name changes, spin-offs) usually move price, not value**, unless a specific cash-flow/risk channel is identified (Apple's stock split, Damodaran suspects, moved price more than its $17B bond issue; Alphabet's rename; HP's breakup).
4. **"Story stocks" require an explicit narrative menu, not discount-rate haggling.** Tesla and Uber show that most of the disagreement in young/disruptive-company valuation lives in narrative choices (market definition, network effects, competitive durability), not in the DCF mechanics — a crowd-valuation or narrative-axis table makes that explicit and falsifiable.
5. **A "big market" doesn't guarantee big winners.** Uber, Snap, and the Facebook/Twitter "postscript" all show that summing the revenue each portfolio company needs to justify its price against a single shared, finite TAM is a required sanity check before holding several names in one hot sector.
6. **Corporate-governance and qualitative factors belong in a DCF only through a specific cash-flow or risk channel** (the "It Proposition," applied to Alibaba, Theranos, Snap) — otherwise they're arbitrary premiums/discounts; modeling governance as an option-like premium/discount that grows with uncertainty and horizon is one disciplined way to do it.
7. **Declining/"Walking Dead" companies (Blackberry, Yahoo, Microsoft) need harvest-and-return management, not a visionary turnaround bet** — continued reinvestment below the cost of capital is what keeps a "cheap" stock cheap forever (the value-trap pattern).
8. **Scandal-driven price collapses (Volkswagen, Deutsche Bank, Valeant) reward a specific method**: back out the market's implied pre-event assumptions, then add deliberately conservative, individually justified cost layers (fines, recalls/legal, reputational lost sales) with an outside reference case for scale — but distinguish reputational damage that blocks a recovery *strategy* (Valeant) from a temporary, recoverable shock (VW, Deutsche Bank).
9. **Politically/family-controlled companies with dual-class shares (Petrobras, Alibaba, Ferrari, Vale) recur as the setting for the worst value destruction** — control separated from economic ownership removes the accountability mechanism that would otherwise stop bad capital allocation.
10. **User/subscriber-based (disaggregated) valuation and aggregated DCF are cross-checks, not alternatives** (Uber, Amazon Prime) — they should converge if built on consistent assumptions, and disaggregation is most useful for isolating exactly which single assumption (e.g., shipping-cost growth vs. revenue growth) determines whether a business/product creates or destroys value.
11. **Classify a "big name" investor's type before their trade changes your view** (Apple/Icahn/Buffett, Valeant/Ackman): an insider or activist can rationally shift your value estimate; a trader shifts only price/momentum; another value investor is a catalyst for an existing gap, not new information about its size.
12. **When a position moves against you, decompose the bad news before concluding your thesis is broken** (Valeant, Vale) — a loss driven by non-cash catch-up or a cyclical/macro reassessment is different from fresh, controllable deterioration.
13. **Position-sizing discipline (never >5–10% in one high-conviction, high-uncertainty bet — >10% of the portfolio in Apple would be "foolhardy"; ≤5% in any new stock) is what allows the strategy of "go where it's darkest" (Vale, Lukoil, Valeant, Deutsche Bank) to survive being wrong** some of the time ([Are you a value investor?](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2013/01/are-you-value-investor-apple-test.html), 2013-01; [Vale Chronicles](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2015/09/no-mas-no-mas-vale-chronicles-continued.html), 2015-09).
14. **Narrative-driven mispricing has repeatable warning signs**: a "runaway story" too compelling to question (Theranos), a cultivated founder-cult image, and a rubber-stamp board with prestige but no domain expertise.
